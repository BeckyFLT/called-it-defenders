{{short description|English local election}}
The '''1954 [[County Borough of Leeds|Leeds]] municipal elections''' were held on Thursday 14 May 1954,<ref name="NYT">{{cite news |title=British Laborites Widen Poll Gains |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1954/05/14/archives/british-laborites-widen-poll-gains-add-510-seats-in-395-areas-early.html |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=14 May 1954 |accessdate=2 September 2012}}</ref> with one third of the council up for election.<ref name="results">{{cite news |title=Municipal results: Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=15 May 1954 }}</ref>

Despite a further swing away from [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] to the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] of 3.7%,<ref name="sharpe">{{cite book |first=L.J. |last=Sharpe  |year=1967 |title=Voting in cities: the 1964 borough elections}}</ref> leaving both parties neck and neck in votes, Labour continued on to their third and final gains unabated in the wards of [[Armley]], [[Blenheim, Leeds|Blenheim]], [[Bramley, Leeds|Bramley]], [[Cross Gates]], [[Stanningley]], Westfield, and [[Woodhouse, Leeds|Woodhouse]], comfortably winning the majority of seats. The swing however helped the Conservatives to hold on to [[Wortley, Leeds|Wortley]], unlike previous years.<ref name="results" /> As such Labour doubled its majority on the council as part of a victorious night on the whole for them.<ref name="NYT" /> Turnout for the election sunk below the 40% mark for the first time in the post-war period, to 39.2%.<ref name="sharpe" />
