The '''2010 Amber Valley Borough Council election''' took place on 6 May 2010 to elect members of [[Amber Valley]] [[Borough status in the United Kingdom|Borough]] Council in [[Derbyshire]], England. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name="change">{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/shared/election2010/council/html/3650.stm|title=Amber Valley|publisher=BBC News|access-date=17 May 2011|date=19 April 2009}}</ref>

After the election, the composition of the council was:
*[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] 29
*[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] 14
*[[British National Party]] 2<ref name=local>{{cite news| url = https://www.theguardian.com/society/table/2010/may/07/local-elections | title = Local elections 2010 | access-date = 2011-05-17 | date = 2010-05-07 | work = The Guardian | location=London}}</ref>

==Campaign==
15 seats were contested in the election by a total of 50 candidates.<ref name="valley">{{cite news|title=Amber Valley Borough Council|date=28 April 2010|work=[[Derby Telegraph]]|page=15}}</ref>

The Conservatives campaigned on their record of controlling the council for the previous 10 years, pointing to keeping [[council tax]] increases low and plans to [[Urban renewal|regenerate]] local towns.<ref name="impact">{{cite news|title=Be prepared to vote in the other election that will have big impact on local services|last=Green|first=Kirsty|author2=Jones, Chris|date=28 April 2010|work=[[Derby Telegraph]]|page=14}}</ref> However [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] pledged to appoint people as area managers who would be a contact point for people to bring any problems and planned to use [[compulsory purchase order]]s to bring empty properties into use.<ref name=impact/> Meanwhile, the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] targeted seats in [[Belper]] and [[Duffield, Derbyshire|Duffield]] campaigning on regeneration, [[recycling]] and litter, while the [[British National Party]] hoped to increase on the 2 seats they held in [[Heanor]].<ref name=impact/>

==Election result==
The results saw no change in the party balance as Labour held the 8 seats they had been defending and the Conservatives the other 7 seats, meaning the Conservatives remained in control of the council.<ref name="success">{{cite news|title=Amber Valley Tories follow MP's success by retaining council seats|last=Jones|first=Caroline|date=8 May 2010|work=[[Derby Telegraph]]|page=4}}</ref> Neither the Liberal Democrats or British National Party won any seats, with the British National Party vote dropping in Heanor where it held 2 seats.<ref name=success/>
