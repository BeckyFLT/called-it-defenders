The '''1998 Wandsworth Council election''' took place on 7 May 1998 to elect members of [[London Borough of Wandsworth|Wandsworth London Borough Council]] in [[London]], [[England]]. The whole council was up for election and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] Party stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name="London 1998 election">{{cite web|title=London Borough Council Elections 7th May 1998|url=http://londondatastore-upload.s3.amazonaws.com/docs/LBCE_1998-5-7.pdf|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150222014659/https://londondatastore-upload.s3.amazonaws.com/docs/LBCE_1998-5-7.pdf|url-status=dead|archive-date=22 February 2015|website=London Datastore|publisher=London Research Centre|access-date=5 February 2024}}</ref>

At the same time as the election Wandsworth saw 74.3% vote in favour of the [[1998 Greater London Authority referendum]] and 25.7% against, on a 38.7% [[Voter turnout|turnout]].<ref name=mayor>{{cite news | title = Policy and politics: Local elections: How London voted for a mayor | newspaper = [[The Guardian]] | page = 16 | date = 1998-05-09 }}</ref>

==Campaign==

In the run up to the election a private Conservative [[opinion poll|poll]] was leaked which showed them 17% behind [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] and likely to only win in 4 [[Wards of the United Kingdom|wards]] as compared to 18 for Labour.<ref name=losses>{{cite news | first = Andrew | last = Pierce | title = Tories face big losses in local elections | newspaper = [[The Times]] | page = 2 | date = 1998-04-14 }}</ref> However Labour and the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] accused the Conservatives of leaking the poll in order to lower expectations.<ref name=bogus>{{cite news | first = Charles | last = Reiss | title = Tories accused of bogus leak in London poll war | newspaper = [[Evening Standard]] | page = 17 | date = 1998-04-14 }}</ref> The Conservatives would have lost control of the council on a 7% swing from the [[1994 Wandsworth Council election|1994 election]],<ref name=fear>{{cite web| url = http://www.lgcplus.com/news/tories-fear-for-wandsworth/1463626.article | title = Tories fear for Wandsworth | access-date = 2009-08-31 | date = 1998-05-05 | publisher = [[Local Government Chronicle]]}}</ref> or if the [[1997 United Kingdom general election|1997 general election results]] in the area were repeated.<ref name=test>{{cite news | first = Peter | last = Kellner | title = First test for new parties | newspaper = [[Evening Standard]] | page = 22 | date = 1998-04-15 }}</ref> However Wandsworth had low [[council tax]] levels,  the lowest in the country, and the Conservatives had recently cut the tax by 24%.<ref name=war>{{cite news | title = Councils of war: the 12 key town halls they're fighting for | newspaper = [[Evening Standard]] | page = 19 | date = 1998-05-06 }}</ref> This meant commentators expected the Conservatives to stay in control of what was described as their showpiece council.<ref name=test/><ref name=showpiece>{{cite news | first = Alexandra | last = Frean | title = Tories rally round showpiece council in election countdown | newspaper = [[The Times]] | page = 12 | date = 1998-04-17 }}</ref>

For the election 9 of the 22 wards that made up the council were seen as being marginal.<ref name=showpiece/>

==Election result==
The results saw the Conservatives increase their control of the council gaining an extra 5 seats.<ref name=comfort>{{cite news | first = Fran | last = Abrams | title = A day from which all parties took comfort | newspaper = [[The Independent]] | page = 11 | date = 1998-05-08 }}</ref> This meant that with 50 seats the Conservatives had the most seats they had yet had on the council.<ref name=guessing>{{cite news| url = http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/events/local_elections_98/news/89402.stm | title = Voters leave parties guessing | access-date = 2009-08-31 | date = 1998-05-08 | work = [[BBC News Online]]}}</ref> Labour lost seats in St John and Roehampton wards, which was put down to the closure of the local [[Queen Mary's Hospital, Roehampton|Queen Mary's Hospital]].<ref name=score>{{cite news | first = Cathy | last = Comerford | title = Tories score flagship victory with low taxes: Wandsworth | newspaper = [[Evening Standard]] | page = 4 | date = 1998-05-08 }}</ref> Voter turnout at 40% was 10% down on the previous 1994 election.<ref name=score/>
