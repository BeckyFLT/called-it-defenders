The '''2023 Windsor and Maidenhead Borough Council election''' took place on 4 May 2023 to elect members of [[Windsor and Maidenhead Borough Council]] in [[Berkshire]], England.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rbwm.gov.uk/home/council-and-democracy/elections-and-voting/elections-2023/borough-elections-2023/notice-elections-borough |title=Notice of elections – Borough |date=21 March 2023 |access-date=16 April 2023 |publisher=[[Windsor and Maidenhead Borough Council]]}}</ref> This was on the same day as other [[2023 United Kingdom local elections|local elections]]. The election saw the Liberal Democrats win a majority of the seats on the council, which had previously been under Conservative control.

==Overview==
Windsor and Maidenhead Borough Council had been under Conservative majority control since 2007. The Conservative leader of the council was Andrew Johnson, who had been leader since 2019. Prior to the election, there were six registered political parties represented on the council (two of which were Residents' Associations) and one independent councillor. The Old Windsor Residents' Association, The Borough First Independents, and the one independent councillor had all formed a political group called the "Local Independents", led by Lynne Jones.<ref>{{cite web |title=Your councillors |url=https://rbwm.moderngov.co.uk/mgMemberIndex.aspx?FN=PARTY&VW=LIST&PIC=0 |website=Royal Borough of Windsor and Maidenhead |access-date=4 May 2023}}</ref> There was one vacancy on the council immediately before the election, where a Liberal Democrat councillor for Belmont ward had resigned in March 2023.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Bagley |first1=James |title=Probed Maidenhead councillor resigns following 'outburst' |url=https://www.windsorobserver.co.uk/news/23415812.probed-maidenhead-councillor-resigns-following-outburst/ |access-date=5 May 2023 |work=The Royal Borough Observer |date=27 March 2023}}</ref>

The election saw the Liberal Democrats win 22 of the 41 seats on the council, up from 10 before the election, giving them an overall majority. The Conservatives saw their number of seats fall from 22 to 7.<ref>{{cite web |title=Borough elections 2023 |url=https://scripts.rbwm.gov.uk/scripts/elections/2023/ |website=Royal Borough of Windsor and Maidenhead |access-date=5 May 2023}}</ref> One of the Conservatives to lose their seat was the leader of the council, Andrew Johnson.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Almroth-Wright |first1=Indy |last2=O'Hagan |first2=Patrick |title=Local elections 2023: Leader ousted by 22-year-old as Tories lose control |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-berkshire-65487770 |access-date=5 May 2023 |work=BBC News |date=5 May 2023}}</ref>

The Liberal Democrat leader, Simon Werner, was formally appointed leader of the council at the subsequent annual council meeting on 23 May 2023.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Oricchio |first1=Elisa |title=New Royal Borough leader and cabinet confirmed at meeting |url=https://www.maidenhead-advertiser.co.uk/gallery/windsor/185700/new-royal-borough-leader-and-cabinet-has-been-announced.html |access-date=31 May 2023 |work=Maidenhead Advertiser |date=25 May 2023}}</ref> The Conservatives appointed Maureen Hunt to be their new leader in opposition.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Preston |first1=James |title=New Conservative leader says 'time to rebuild' in Windsor and Maidenhead |url=https://www.maidenhead-advertiser.co.uk/gallery/windsor/185550/new-conservative-leader-says-time-to-rebuild-in-windsor-and-maidenhead.html |access-date=31 May 2023 |work=Maidenhead Advertiser |date=18 May 2023}}</ref>
