The '''2022 Wandsworth London Borough Council elections''' took place on 5 May 2022.

All 58 members of [[Wandsworth London Borough Council]] were elected. The elections took place alongside [[2022 London local elections|local elections in the other London boroughs]] and [[2022 United Kingdom local elections|elections to local authorities across the United Kingdom]].

In the [[2018 Wandsworth London Borough Council election|previous election in 2018]], the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] maintained their longstanding control of the council, winning 33 out of the 60 seats with the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]] forming the principal opposition with 26 of the remaining 27 seats. The 2022 election took place under new election boundaries, which reduced the number of councillors to 58. Labour won control for the first time since 1978.

== Background ==

=== History ===
[[File:Wandsworth London UK local election 2018 map.svg|thumb|Result of the 2018 borough election]]
The thirty-two [[London boroughs]] were established in 1965 by the [[London Government Act 1963]]. They are the principal authorities in Greater London and have responsibilities including education, housing, planning, highways, social services, libraries, recreation, waste, environmental health and revenue collection. Some of the powers are shared with the [[Greater London Authority]], which also manages passenger transport, police, and fire.<ref>{{Cite web|title=The essential guide to London local government {{!}} London Councils|url=https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/who-runs-london/essential-guide-london-local-government|access-date=2021-10-10|website=www.londoncouncils.gov.uk}}</ref>

Wandsworth was alternately under [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] and [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] control in the elections after its creation, and subsequently has been under Conservative control since the [[1978 Wandsworth London Borough Council election|1978 election]]. In the [[2018 Wandsworth London Borough Council election|most recent election in 2018]], the council was considered a key target for Labour in London.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Hill|first=Dave|date=2018-01-22|title=Can Labour take control of Tory flagship Wandsworth Council?|url=https://www.onlondon.co.uk/can-labour-take-control-of-tory-flagship-wandsworth-council/|access-date=2021-10-22|website=OnLondon|language=en-GB}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|last=Mance|first=Henry|date=2018-04-01|title=Labour eyeing historic victory in Tory flagship council Wandsworth|work=Financial Times|url=https://www.ft.com/content/5d94c22c-333e-11e8-b5bf-23cb17fd1498|access-date=2021-10-22}}</ref> The Conservatives held the council, winning 33 seats with 38.3% of the vote, while Labour won 26 seats with 38.7% of the vote. The independent candidate Malcolm Grimston was also elected.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Frodsham|first=Isobel|date=2018-05-04|title=Jubilant Tories cling on to win crown jewel stakes in Wandsworth|url=https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/local-elections-2018-jubilant-tories-cling-on-to-win-crown-jewel-stakes-in-wandsworth-after-early-scare-at-count-a3831381.html|access-date=2021-10-22|website=www.standard.co.uk|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Wandsworth London Borough Council|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/topics/cv8k1e8g5dwt|access-date=2021-10-22|website=BBC News|language=en-GB}}</ref>

=== Council term ===

Candida Jones, a Labour councillor for Furzedown, resigned in 2019 due to taking a politically restricted job. Graham Loveland held the seat for Labour in the subsequent by-election, with the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] increasing their share of the vote to come in second place.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Baston|first=Lewis|date=2019-06-22|title=Wandsworth: Labour holds Furzedown ward, but swing to Lib Dems confirms trend|url=https://www.onlondon.co.uk/wandsworth-labour-holds-furzedown-ward-but-swing-to-lib-dems-confirms-trend/|access-date=2021-10-22|website=OnLondon|language=en-GB}}</ref> A Labour councillor for Bedford ward, [[Fleur Anderson]], resigned in April 2021 having been elected as MP for [[Putney (UK Parliament constituency)|Putney]] in the [[2019 United Kingdom general election|2019 general election]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Bayley|first=Sian|date=2021-04-09|title=Putney MP stands down as local councillor|url=https://www.mylondon.news/news/south-london-news/putney-mp-stands-down-local-20354503|access-date=2021-10-22|website=MyLondon|language=en}}</ref> A by-election to fill the seat was held on 6 May 2021 alongside the [[2021 London mayoral election]] and [[2021 London Assembly election|London Assembly election]], which was won by the Labour candidate Hannah Stanislaus.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2021-05-08|title=London Elections 2021 Day Two: By-election round-up|url=https://www.swlondoner.co.uk/news/08052021-london-elections-2021-day-2-by-election-round-up/|access-date=2021-10-22|website=South West Londoner|language=en-GB}}</ref> In August 2021, Stanislaus resigned from the Labour Party to sit as independent, saying that she had "been bullied out" and that the party whip had made a personal attack against her in a report.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Balham councillor resigns from Labour party after bullying accusations|url=https://www.wandsworthguardian.co.uk/news/19539266.balham-councillor-resigns-labour-party/|access-date=2021-10-22|website=Wandsworth Times|date=26 August 2021 |language=en}}</ref> She later resigned as a councillor, with a by-election held on 25 November. The Labour candidate Sheila Boswell held it for the party with a majority of a single vote over the Conservative candidate.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Mayer|first=James|date=2021-11-29|title=Labour wins crucial South London council election by a single vote|url=https://www.mylondon.news/news/south-london-news/labour-wins-crucial-london-council-22305449|access-date=2021-11-30|website=MyLondon|language=en}}</ref>

Along with most London boroughs, Wandsworth was electing councillors under new ward boundaries in 2022.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Bayley|first=Sian|date=2019-10-29|title=Parts of Wandsworth are expanding so much its ward maps will have to be re-drawn|url=https://www.mylondon.news/news/west-london-news/parts-wandsworth-expanding-much-ward-17166597|access-date=2021-10-22|website=MyLondon|language=en}}</ref> Following local consultation, the [[Local Government Boundary Commission for England]] produced new boundaries reducing the number of councillors from 60 to 58 across fourteen three-councillor wards and eight two-seat wards.<ref>{{Cite web|title=LGBCE {{!}} Wandsworth {{!}} LGBCE Site|url=http://www.lgbce.org.uk/all-reviews/greater-london/greater-london/wandsworth|access-date=2021-10-22|website=www.lgbce.org.uk|language=en}}</ref>

== Electoral process ==
Wandsworth, as with all other London borough councils, elects all of its councillors at once every four years, with the previous election having taken place in 2018. The election took place by multi-member [[first-past-the-post voting]], with each ward being represented by two or three councillors. Electors had as many votes as there were councillors to be elected in their ward, with the top two or three being elected.

All [[Elections in the United Kingdom#Registration procedure|registered electors]] ([[British citizen|British]], [[Irish citizen|Irish]], [[Commonwealth citizen|Commonwealth]] and [[European Union]] citizens) living in London aged 18 or over were entitled to vote in the election. People who lived at two addresses in different councils, such as university students with different term-time and holiday addresses, were entitled to be registered for and vote in elections in both local authorities.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web|title=How the elections work {{!}} London Councils|url=https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/who-runs-london/london-elections/how-elections-work|access-date=2021-09-14|website=www.londoncouncils.gov.uk}}</ref> Voting in-person at polling stations took place from 7:00 to 22:00 on election day, and voters were able to apply for postal votes or proxy votes in advance of the election.<ref name=":1" />
