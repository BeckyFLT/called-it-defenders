{{Short description|1987 UK local government election}}
{{Use dmy dates|date=March 2020}}
{{Use British English|date=March 2020}}
[[File:Wigan85.png|thumb|500px|right|Map of the results of the 1987 Wigan council election.]]
Elections to the [[Metropolitan Borough of Wigan|Wigan]] council were held on Thursday, 7 May 1987, with one third of the council up for election. Previous to the election there had been two by-elections held, resulting in a [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] gain of the seat being fought in Swinley from the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] and Labour retaining one of their Hindley Green seats. Participation for this election increased substantially from the [[1986 Wigan Council election|previous year]]'s lows. Only one ward went unopposed (Leigh Central) as Labour reliably provided a full-slate of candidates, the [[SDP-Liberal Alliance|Alliance]] all but [[Atherton, Greater Manchester|Atherton]] and the Conservatives up from 16 to 18, by way of fielding candidates in the Labour strongholds of Abram and [[Worsley Mesnes]] this time around. An [[Independent Labour]] candidate also fought and won [[Hindley, Greater Manchester|Hindley]], with an Independent victory last seen in [[1976 Wigan Council election|1976]], and Independent representation in [[1980 Wigan Council election|1980]], when that same victor failed to survive the re-warding.

The results seen an end to four years of consecutive increases in Labour's share, peaking with last year's all-time high, as they slipped back into a respectable high-fifties and their loss dispersing to each of the opposition parties. Labour lost four seats in total, with the aforementioned Independent Labour loss in Hindley, an Alliance casualty from last year's elections re-elected in Winstanley, another loss to Alliance in [[Aspull]]-[[Standish, Greater Manchester|Standish]] and the Conservatives comfortable retaking the seat in Swinley they'd lost in the earlier mentioned by-election. These losses were offset somewhat by a gain from Alliance in Tyldesley East, returning the ward to entire Labour representation. Overall turnout rose to an above-average 37.1%.<ref name="results">{{cite web | url = http://www.wigan.gov.uk/NR/rdonlyres/3692F771-E969-41EE-B068-1F9F20DFE0B7/0/WiganResults1973to2007.pdf | title = Wigan Council results from 1973 to 2008 | publisher = wigan.gov.uk | access-date = 2012-01-30 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20111120022015/http://www.wigan.gov.uk/NR/rdonlyres/3692F771-E969-41EE-B068-1F9F20DFE0B7/0/WiganResults1973to2007.pdf | archive-date = 2011-11-20 | url-status = dead }}</ref>
