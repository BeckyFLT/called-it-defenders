[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] in red, [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] in yellow, [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] in blue, [[Independent (politician)|independents]] in grey and East Cleveland Independents in pink.]]
An election took place on 1 May 2003 to elect members of [[Redcar and Cleveland]] [[Unitary authorities of England|Unitary]] Council in England. The whole council was up for election with boundary changes since the [[1999 Redcar and Cleveland Council election|last election in 1999]].<ref name="lose">{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/shared/bsp/hi/vote2003/locals/html/210.stm|title=Local elections|publisher=BBC News|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref> The [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour party]] lost overall control of the council to [[no overall control]].<ref name="council">{{cite news|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/uknews/1429056/Local-council-election-results.html|title=Local council election results|date=3 May 2003|work=The Daily Telegraph|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref>

==Background==
Between 1999 and 2003 the [[Local Government Boundary Commission for England]] had made changes to the [[Wards of the United Kingdom|wards]] of the council.<ref name="line">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/04/14/The+North+East+Archive/7034818.Candidates_line_up/|title=Candidates line up|date=14 April 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref> The changes included renaming Belmont ward to Westworth, and Redcar ward to Zetland, with 18 of the 22 wards having boundary changes.<ref name=line/>

Before the election Labour ran the council with 31 seats, compared to 14 [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]], 11 [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] and 3 East Cleveland Independents.<ref name=line/> In total 151 candidates stood for the 59 seats that were being contested,<ref name=line/> an increase of 9 from the 1999 election.<ref name="standing">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/04/04/The+North+East+Archive/7035897.151_standing_for_council/|title=151 standing for council|date=4 April 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref>

The election had a trial of all [[postal voting]] to try and increase [[Voter turnout|turnout]],<ref name="missing">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/04/25/The+North+East+Archive/7033337.Voters_go_missing__in_election_run_up/|title=Voters go missing in election run-up|date=25 April 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref> with [[ballot]]s being delivered to voters 2 weeks before the election.<ref name=line/>

==Election result==
The count for [[Brotton]] ward was suspended on election night after 2 recounts and completed the following day.<ref name="recount">{{cite web|url=http://www.redcar-cleveland.gov.uk/PressRel.nsf/Published/1E409DB57ADB388380256D1A0029F8F1?OpenDocument|title=Brotton ward election recount today|date=2 May 2003|publisher=Redcar & Cleveland Borough Council|access-date=29 October 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171206201530/http://www.redcar-cleveland.gov.uk/PressRel.nsf/Published/1E409DB57ADB388380256D1A0029F8F1?OpenDocument|archive-date=6 December 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> Overall turnout at the election was 51.5%, an increase from 37% in 1999.<ref name=recount/>

Labour lost their majority on the council, finishing with 23 seats, 7 short of a majority.<ref name="drama">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/05/03/The+North+East+Archive/7032308.High_drama_as_leader_is_beaten_and_breakaway_group_celebrates/|title=High drama as leader is beaten and breakaway group celebrates|date=3 May 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref> Among the Labour [[councillor]]s to lose seats was the leader of the council Dave Walsh in [[Eston]] ward,<ref name=drama/><ref name="unite">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/05/09/The+North+East+Archive/7031568.Party_leaders_set_to_unite/|title=Party leaders set to unite|date=9 May 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref> after he had moved to contest Eston, instead of [[Loftus, North Yorkshire|Loftus]] which he had previously been a councillor for.<ref name="deputy">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/05/09/The+North+East+Archive/7031577.Deputy_voted_into_ousted_leader_s_shoes/|title=Deputy voted into ousted leader's shoes|date=9 May 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref>

Following the election Labour chose George Dunning, formerly the deputy leader, as the new leader of the party on the council, defeating Dave McLuckie by 12 votes to 11.<ref name=deputy/> Meanwhile, the 8 independents joined, with Steve Kay as their leader, in an East Cleveland, Eston and [[Marske-by-the-Sea|Marske]] Independents group.<ref name=unite/> After negotiations the Liberal Democrat, Conservative and Independent groups formed a coalition to take control of the council from Labour.<ref name="grip">{{cite news|url=http://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/archive/2003/05/12/The+North+East+Archive/7031407.Labour_loses_its_grip_on_council/|title=Labour loses its grip on council|date=12 May 2003|work=[[The Northern Echo]]|access-date=29 October 2011}}</ref>
