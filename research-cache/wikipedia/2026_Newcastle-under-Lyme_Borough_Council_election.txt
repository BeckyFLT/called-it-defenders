The '''2026 Newcastle-under-Lyme Borough Council election''' was held on Thursday 7 May 2026 to elect all 44 councillors who serve on [[Newcastle-under-Lyme Borough Council]]. It was held [[2026 United Kingdom local elections|alongside council elections across England]].

Prior to the election, the council was under [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] majority control. [[Reform UK]], which had held no seats on the council immediately before the election, won 27 seats, giving them an overall majority. The Conservatives won 15 seats and [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] won 2 seats.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Price |first1=Richard |last2=Aitken |first2=Jennie |title=Reform gains control of Newcastle-under-Lyme in historic night for party |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwy2e2w7y46o |access-date=8 May 2026 |publisher=BBC News |date=8 May 2026 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114400/https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwy2e2w7y46o |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> Reform chose [[Jonathan Gullis]] to be its new group leader after the election.<ref>{{cite web |last=Price |first=Richard |date=14 May 2026 |title=Ex-Tory MP named council's new Reform group leader |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ce8p74n52ypo |website=BBC News |location=West Midlands |access-date=14 May 2026 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807105300/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ce8p74n52ypo |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> He was formally appointed as the new leader of the council at the subsequent annual council meeting on 20 May 2026.<ref>{{cite web |title=Council minutes, 20 May 2026 |url=https://moderngov.newcastle-staffs.gov.uk/ieListDocuments.aspx?CId=152&MId=3873 |website=Newcastle under Lyme Borough Council |access-date=6 September 2026}}</ref>

== Background ==
Simon Tagg had served as Leader of Newcastle-under-Lyme Borough Council since December 2017. In the [[2025 Staffordshire County Council election]], Newcastle-under-Lyme experienced a significant political shift towards [[Reform UK|Reform]],<ref>{{Cite web |last=Andrews |first=Rob |date=2025-05-02 |title=Live: Reform UK win eight out of nine council seats across Newcastle |url=https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/live-updates-keele-nine-seats-10149986 |access-date=2025-11-13 |website=Stoke on Trent Live |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114400/https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/live-updates-keele-nine-seats-10149986 |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> which secured eight of the nine available seats in the area and 44.7% of the total vote. [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] failed to win any seats, while the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] retained only one.

Reform gained its first seat on the council earlier in May 2025 through a by-election victory in [[Knutton]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Sutton |first=Jonathan |date=2025-05-02 |title=Reform UK double delight as Lynn Dean wins May Bank & Wolstanton |url=https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/reform-uk-double-delight-lynn-10146234 |access-date=2025-11-13 |website=Stoke-on-Trent Live |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114409/https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/reform-uk-double-delight-lynn-10146234 |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> Lynn Dean, the elected councillor for Knutton, had the whip suspended by Reform UK in late March 2026 following allegations of racist comments made on social media, uncovered by the anti-racism campaign group Hope Not Hate; Dean said she had already decided to leave the party.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Price |first=Richard |date=2026-03-27 |title=Reform councillor suspended following racism row |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5y7ezxqx04o |access-date=2026-03-27 |website=BBC News |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114438/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5y7ezxqx04o |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> She resigned as councillor for Knutton on 1 April 2026, weeks before she would have faced re-election, remaining a Staffordshire county councillor for May Bank and Wolstanton; the Knutton seat was left vacant until the 2026 borough election.<ref>{{cite news |last=Corrigan |first=Philip |title=Suspended Reform UK councillor resigns from Newcastle Borough Council |url=https://uk.news.yahoo.com/suspended-reform-uk-councillor-resigns-072456202.html |work=Yahoo News UK |date=1 April 2026 |access-date=3 September 2026}}</ref>

Following his defection from the Conservative Party in December 2025,<ref>{{Cite web |last=Flash |first=Oprah |date=2025-12-01 |title=Ex-deputy chair among Tory defections to Reform |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cgr12e7259xo |access-date=2026-04-01 |website=BBC News |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114512/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cgr12e7259xo |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> [[Jonathan Gullis]], the former [[Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)|MP]] for [[Stoke-on-Trent North]], announced his candidacy for the Kidsgrove and Ravenscliffe ward representing Reform UK.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Corrigan |first=Phil |date=2026-04-01 |title=Ex-MP Jonathan Gullis to stand in election for Reform UK |url=https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/ex-mp-jonathan-gullis-stand-10895645 |access-date=2026-04-01 |website=Stoke on Trent Live |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114545/https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/ex-mp-jonathan-gullis-stand-10895645 |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref>
Additionally, the former MP for [[Stafford (constituency)|Stafford]], [[Jeremy Lefroy]] announced he was standing for candidacy for the Maer and Whitmore ward representing the Conservatives. Both were elected.

A major topic of local concern was the [[2024–present structural changes to local government in England|proposed reorganisation of local government]], with widespread opposition to the government's plans to potentially merge [[Staffordshire Moorlands District Council|Staffordshire Moorlands]], [[Stoke-on-Trent City Council|Stoke-on-Trent]], and Newcastle-under-Lyme councils.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-11-08 |title=Five different plans for re-drawing Staffordshire's political map |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c4gplen51klo |access-date=2025-11-13 |website=BBC News |language=en-GB |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114400/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c4gplen51klo |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> A large number of residents had signed a petition protesting these proposals due to the prospect of inheriting the large debt accrued by Stoke-on-Trent. <ref>{{Cite web |last=Andrews |first=Rob |date=2025-01-20 |title='No to merger with Stoke' protest in Newcastle borough independence fight |url=https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/no-merger-stoke-protest-newcastle-9877564 |access-date=2025-11-13 |website=Stoke-on-Trent Live |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114400/https://www.stokesentinel.co.uk/news/stoke-on-trent-news/no-merger-stoke-protest-newcastle-9877564 |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> Despite these efforts, the merger was confirmed in July 2026.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Aitken |first=Jen |date=2026-07-18 |title=What council shake-up could mean for Staffordshire |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80nre2kx39o |access-date=2026-08-06 |website=BBC News |language=en |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260807114400/https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c80nre2kx39o |archive-date=7 August 2026}}</ref> 
The council is set to be abolished in April 2028.
