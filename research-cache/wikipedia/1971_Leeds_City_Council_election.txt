The '''1971 [[County Borough of Leeds|Leeds]] municipal elections''' were held on 14 May 1971,<ref name="NYT">{{cite news |title=British Laborites Sweep Local Voting|url=https://select.nytimes.com/gst/abstract.html?res=FB071EFE3954127B93C7A8178ED85F458785F9 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=15 May 1971 |access-date=21 October 2012}}</ref> with one third of the councillors up for vote including a double vacancy in Talbot.<ref name="results">{{cite news |title=Municipal results: Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=15 May 1971 }}</ref>

Reflecting national patterns,<ref name="NYT" /> the results were a near mirror-image of the [[1968 Leeds City Council election|1968 results]], as [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] achieved their highest vote share since [[1945 Leeds City Council election|1945]], and their greatest number of votes since [[1953 Leeds City Council election|1953]]. It was the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] who sunk to a low-point this time around, setting post-war record lows in both vote percentages and actual votes. However, with the defeat being less severe and the entirety of the council not being elected this time, the Conservatives held on to control with the superior number of aldermen allotted to them the [[1970 Leeds City Council election|previous year]].<ref name="results" /> Overall turnout across the city was 37.8%.<ref>{{cite news |title=Labour sweeps in with 20 majority in Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=6 May 1972 }} Scan {{Deprecated archive|url=https://archive.today/20130131100930/http://i1066.photobucket.com/albums/u413/Hullenedge/WYorks72001.jpg|title=here}}</ref>

Starting from such a shrunken base following their overwhelming defeat in 1968, Labour gained over half of seats up for election, as well as holding the seats in the four wards they won previously ([[Leeds city centre|City]], East [[Hunslet]], [[Holbeck]] and [[Middleton, Leeds|Middleton]]). Labour gained seventeen in total, with all but Castleton from the Conservatives: [[Armley (ward)|Armley]], [[Beeston, Leeds|Beeston]], [[Bramley, Leeds|Bramley]], [[Burley, Leeds|Burley]], [[Burmantofts]], Castleton, [[Gipton]], [[Harehills]], [[Kirkstall (ward)|Kirkstall]], [[Osmondthorpe]], [[Richmond Hill, Leeds|Richmond Hill]], Scott Hall, [[Seacroft]], [[Stanningley]], [[Whinmoor]], [[Woodhouse, Leeds|Woodhouse]] and [[Wortley, Leeds|Wortley]]. Castleton was gained from the [[Liberal Party (UK)|Liberals]], who repeated their success in West Hunslet for the third year running and gained from the Conservatives, leaving their total unchanged.<ref name="results" />
