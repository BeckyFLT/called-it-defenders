The '''2024 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election''' took place on Thursday 2 May 2024 to elect all 72 seats to [[Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council|Dudley Council]], alongside the [[2024 United Kingdom local elections|other local elections across the United Kingdom]] being held on the same day.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Types of elections |url=https://www.dudley.gov.uk/council-community/elections/types-of-elections/ |access-date=2024-01-02 |website=www.dudley.gov.uk |language=en}}</ref>

Prior to the election the council had been under Conservative majority control since the [[2021 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2021 election]].

The Conservatives lost control of the council, with no party having an overall majority and Labour and the Conservatives ending up with the same number of seats.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2024-05-03 |title="Dudley Council election results in full: Tories lose control as they end up level with Labour" |url=https://www.expressandstar.com/news/local-hubs/dudley/2024/05/03/dudley-council-election-results-as-first-wards-are-announced/ |url-status= |work=Express and Star}}</ref> After a week of negotiations between the Conservative and Labour groups, it was announced that the existing Conservative leader, Patrick Harley, would remain leader of the council (and therefore appoint the council's cabinet) while Labour would be in charge of the majority of the council's scrutiny committee chairmanships and the mayoralty.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6pypj4gy7ro|title=Council remains Conservative-led after deal struck|date=15 May 2024|access-date=24 May 2024|first1=Martyn|publisher=[[BBC News]]|last1=Smith}}</ref>

== Background ==

=== History ===
The [[Local Government Act 1972]] created a two-tier system of metropolitan counties and districts covering [[Greater Manchester]], [[Merseyside]], [[South Yorkshire]], [[Tyne and Wear]], the [[West Midlands (county)|West Midlands]], and [[West Yorkshire]] starting in 1974. Dudley was a district of the West Midlands metropolitan county.<ref name=guide>{{cite book |title=Local Government in England and Wales: A Guide to the New System |year=1974 |publisher=[[HMSO]] |location=London |isbn=0-11-750847-0 |page=7 }}</ref> The [[Local Government Act 1985]] abolished the metropolitan county councils, with metropolitan districts taking on most of their powers as [[metropolitan borough]]s. The [[West Midlands Combined Authority]] was created in 2016 and began electing the [[mayor of the West Midlands]] from 2017, which was given strategic powers covering a region coterminous with the West Midlands metropolitan county.<ref>{{cite web|title=CONSTITUTION OF THE WEST MIDLANDS COMBINED AUTHORITY|url=https://www.wmca.org.uk/media/1576/constitution-master-module-2642017.pdf|access-date=4 December 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220531185344/https://www.wmca.org.uk/media/1576/constitution-master-module-2642017.pdf|archive-date=31 May 2022|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Dudley Council has variously been under Labour control, Conservative control and [[no overall control]] since it was established. The Conservatives controlled the council from the [[2004 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2004 election]] until Labour gained control in the [[2012 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2012 election]]. Labour lost overall control in the [[2016 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2016 election]] but continued to lead the council until 2017, when the Conservatives led the council, still without a majority. In the [[2021 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2021 elections]], the Conservatives gained a majority on the council, which they have held since.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Farrington |first=Dayna |title=Full election results from Dudley Council as Conservatives gain power |url=https://www.expressandstar.com/news/politics/elections-west-midlands-mayor-pcc-council-2021/2021/05/07/dudley-councils-election-results--in-full/ |access-date=2022-03-26 |website=www.expressandstar.com |language=en}}</ref>

In the [[2023 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|most recent council election in 2023]], the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] won 44 of the 72 seats, one less than prior to the election, while [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] won 27 seats, increasing their membership on the council by 1.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Dudley result - Local Elections 2023 |language=en-GB |work=BBC News |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/election/2023/england/councils/E08000027 |access-date=2024-01-02}}</ref>

Two [[by-election]]s and three defections between the 2023 and 2024 elections changed the council's composition ahead of this election; see [[2023 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election]] for details.

=== Electoral process ===
The council usually elects members in thirds every year except the 4th in a four-year cycle.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Composition of Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council |url=http://opencouncildata.co.uk/council.php?c=270&y=0 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240102150918/http://opencouncildata.co.uk/council.php?c=270&y=0 |archive-date=2 Jan 2024 |access-date=2 Jan 2024 |website=Open Councill Data UK}}</ref> However, due to a boundary review of the wards by the [[Local Government Boundary Commission for England]], all 72 seats to [[Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council]] were up for election in 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Local Government Boundary Review |url=https://www.dudley.gov.uk/council-community/elections/local-government-boundary-review/ |access-date=2024-01-02 |website=www.dudley.gov.uk |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=LGBCE |title=Dudley {{!}} LGBCE |url=https://www.lgbce.org.uk/all-reviews/dudley |access-date=2024-01-02 |website=www.lgbce.org.uk |language=en}}</ref>
