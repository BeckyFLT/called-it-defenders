The '''2003 Windsor and Maidenhead Borough Council election''' took place on 1 May 2003 to elect members of [[Royal Borough of Windsor and Maidenhead|Windsor and Maidenhead]] [[Unitary authority|Unitary]] Council in [[Berkshire]], England. The whole council was up for election with boundary changes since the [[2000 Windsor and Maidenhead Council election|last election in 2000]] reducing the number of seats by 1.<ref name=gain>{{cite news| url = https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/shared/bsp/hi/vote2003/locals/html/329.stm | title = Local elections | access-date = 2010-11-27 | publisher = BBC News}}</ref> The [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] gained overall control of the council from [[no overall control]].<ref name=english>{{cite news | title = English councils | newspaper = The Times| page = 47 | date = 2003-05-03 }}</ref>

==Campaign==
Windsor and Maidenhead council was a top target for the Liberal Democrats in the [[2003 United Kingdom local elections|2003 local elections]].<ref name=focus>{{cite news | title = Parties try to shift focus to Birmingham from Baghdad | newspaper = Financial Times | page = 10 | date = 2003-04-15 }}</ref> The party had come close to taking the [[Maidenhead (UK Parliament constituency)|Maidenhead parliamentary constituency]] at the last [[2001 United Kingdom general election|general election in 2001]] and were optimistic of taking control of the council.<ref name=mosque>{{cite news | title = Local Elections: Where a mosque is the big issue; Lib Dem target Maidenhead | newspaper = The Independent | page = 6 | date = 2003-04-22 }}</ref><ref name=face>{{cite news | first = Julia | last = Hartley-Brewer | title = Tories face a poll wipe-out | publisher = [[The Express on Sunday]] | date = 2003-04-20 }}</ref> The Liberal Democrats were expected to benefit from the support of the estimated 3,000 Muslims who lived in the council area due to the party's opposition to the [[Iraq War]].<ref name=mosque/> The issue of a proposed extension to a local mosque was also used for the Liberal Democrats,<ref name=mosque/> but the Conservatives accused them of pandering to the Muslim community.<ref name=dirty>{{cite news | first = Richard | last = Milne | title = Tension rises in Maidenhead amid claims of dirty tricks | newspaper = Financial Times | page = 5 | date = 2003-05-01 }}</ref>

The council had been controlled by the Conservatives since the 2000 election in an alliance with 2 of the [[Independent (politician)|independent]] [[councillor]]s.<ref name=overthrow>{{cite news | first = Jill | last = Sherman | title = Lib Dems plotting to overthrow Tories in Windsor | newspaper = The Times| page = 10 | date = 2003-04-24 }}</ref> However the Liberal Democrats attacked the council for having raised [[council tax]] by 9.3% in the last year and by 20% over the 3 years since the last election.<ref name=overthrow/> They also criticised the council for its plans to demolish the town hall and pointed to a weak rating for the council by the [[Audit Commission (United Kingdom)|Audit Commission]].<ref name=overthrow/> However the Conservatives said Windsor and Maidenhead's council tax was still one of the lowest in the southeast and that if the Liberal Democrats implemented the policies in their manifesto it would mean a 27% increase in council tax.<ref name=overthrow/>

Boundary changes which reduced the number of seats by 1, meant both the Conservatives and Liberal Democrats needed 1 less seat to take control of the council.<ref name=dirty/> With the election important to both parties, the [[Chairman of the Conservative Party|Conservative Party Chairman]] and local [[Member of parliament|MP]] [[Theresa May]] and the Liberal Democrat leader [[Charles Kennedy]] both campaigned in the area.<ref name=dirty/>

The council again used a mobile [[polling station]] to go to railway stations and supermarkets in an attempt to increase [[Voter turnout|turnout]].<ref name=turnout>{{cite news| url = https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/politics/2988307.stm | title = New voting methods 'increase turnout' | access-date = 2010-11-28 | date = 2003-04-30 | publisher = BBC News}}</ref>

==Election result==
The results saw the Liberal Democrats take control of the council,<ref name=but>{{cite news | first = Ross | last = Lydall | title = But in the south Tories fail to make big breakthrough | newspaper = Evening Standard|location=London | page = 7 | date = 2003-05-02 }}</ref> after gaining 13 seats.<ref name=how>{{cite news | first = Paul | last = Waugh | title = How Britain voted: Kennedy claims parity with 30% share of vote; Liberal Democrats | newspaper = The Independent | page = 18 | date = 2003-05-03 }}</ref> The election results were seen as giving the Liberal Democrats hope for the next general election in the Maidenhead constituency after the party won 61% of the vote in [[Wards of the United Kingdom|wards]] within the constituency against 33.6% for the Conservatives.<ref name=how/>
