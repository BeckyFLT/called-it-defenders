The '''2010 Huntingdonshire District Council election''' took place on 6 May 2010 to elect members of [[Huntingdonshire]] [[Non-metropolitan district|District]] Council in [[Cambridgeshire]], [[England]]. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name="election2010">{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/shared/election2010/council/html/3706.stm|title=Huntingdonshire|date=11 May 2010|publisher=[[BBC News Online]]|access-date=8 July 2014}}</ref>

After the election, the composition of the council was:
*[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] 37
*[[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] 12
*[[UK Independence Party]] 2
*[[Independent (politician)|Independent]] 1<ref name="local">{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/society/table/2010/may/07/local-elections|title=local elections 2010|date=7 May 2010|work=[[The Guardian]]|access-date=9 July 2014}}</ref>

==Background==
After the [[2008 Huntingdonshire District Council election|last election in 2008]] the Conservatives continued to run the council with 38 [[councillor]]s, while the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] had 12 and there were 2 [[Independent (politician)|independents]].<ref name="elections2008">{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/shared/bsp/hi/elections/local_council/08/html/12ue.stm|title=Huntingdonshire|date=2 May 2008|publisher=[[BBC News Online]]|access-date=9 July 2014}}</ref> However, in July 2009 the [[UK Independence Party]] won their first seat on the council after Peter Reeve won a [[by-election]] for [[Ramsey, Cambridgeshire|Ramsey]] [[Wards and electoral divisions of the United Kingdom|ward]], taking the seat from the Liberal Democrats.<ref name="ukip">{{cite news|url=http://www.cambridge-news.co.uk/News/Election-victory-for-UKIP.htm?id=436127 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20140709183432/http://www.cambridge-news.co.uk/News/Election-victory-for-UKIP.htm?id=436127 |url-status=dead |archive-date=9 July 2014 |title=Election victory for UKIP |date=24 July 2009 |work=[[Cambridge News]] |access-date=9 July 2014 }}</ref> He was then joined by the other Ramsey councillor, Andy Monk, who had been elected at another by-election in April 2009 as a Conservative,<ref name="successful">{{cite news|url=http://www.huntspost.co.uk/hunts-life/successful_poll_day_for_hunts_tories_1_416534|title=Successful poll day for Hunts Tories|date=3 April 2009|work=Hunts Post|access-date=9 July 2014}}</ref> but [[Defection|defected]] to the UK Independence Party in January 2010.<ref name="defection">{{cite news|url=http://www.cambs24.co.uk/news/conservative_district_councillor_announces_his_defection_to_cambs_branch_of_ukip_1_302629|archive-url=https://archive.today/20140711094450/http://www.cambs24.co.uk/news/conservative_district_councillor_announces_his_defection_to_cambs_branch_of_ukip_1_302629|url-status=dead|archive-date=11 July 2014|title=Conservative district councillor announces his defection to Cambs branch of UKIP|date=11 January 2010|publisher=Cambs24|access-date=9 July 2014}}</ref>

Meanwhile, the Liberal Democrats gained a seat from the Conservatives in another by-election in [[Fenstanton]] in February 2010.<ref name="slips">{{cite news|url=https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/politics/lib-dems-and-tories-gain-as-labour-vote-slips-1911951.html |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20220501/https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/politics/lib-dems-and-tories-gain-as-labour-vote-slips-1911951.html |archive-date=1 May 2022 |url-access=subscription |url-status=live|title=Lib Dems and Tories gain as Labour vote slips|date=26 February 2010|agency=[[Press Association]]|work=[[The Independent]]|access-date=9 July 2014}}{{cbignore}}</ref> These changes meant that going into the 2010 election there were 36 Conservative, 12 Liberal Democrat, 2 UK Independence Party and 2 independent councillors.<ref name=election2010/>
