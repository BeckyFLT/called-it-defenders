The '''2003 Leeds City Council election''' took place on 1 May 2003 to elect members of [[City of Leeds]] [[Metropolitan Borough]] Council in [[West Yorkshire]], [[England]]. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]] stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name=hold>{{cite news| url = https://news.bbc.co.uk/1/shared/bsp/hi/vote2003/locals/html/148.stm | title = Local elections | access-date = 6 August 2018|work= [[BBC News Online]]}}</ref>

==Election result==
The results saw Labour just keep a majority on the council with 52 of the 99 seats.<ref name=cling>{{cite news| url = http://www.yorkshireeveningpost.co.uk/news/Labour-just-cling-on.496233.jp | title = Labour just cling on | access-date = 6 August 2018| date = 2 May 2003 |newspaper= [[Yorkshire Evening Post]]}}</ref> They suffered a net loss of 5 seats,<ref name=cling/> including the leader of the council for the past 7 years, Brian Walker, who was defeated by the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] in [[Rothwell, West Yorkshire|Rothwell]] [[Wards of the United Kingdom|ward]].<ref name=baghdad>{{cite news| first = Peter | last = Hetherington | title = English local elections: Labour hit by tax rises and 'Baghdad backlash': England: Disaffection boosts Tories and Lib Dems |newspaper= [[The Guardian]] | page = 16 | date = 3 May 2003 | issn=0261-3077}}</ref> The [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] gained the most seats to become the second largest party on the council with 22 seats, after making 4 gains in [[Aireborough]], [[Temple Newsam (ward)#Councillors since 1973|Halton]], [[Otley]] and [[Wharfedale]] and [[Roundhay]] wards.<ref name=cling/> As well as the gain in Rothwell, the Liberal Democrats also picked up a seat from Labour in [[Burmantofts]] but lost seats in [[Bramley, Leeds|Bramley]] and Otley and Wharfedale to Labour and Conservatives respectively.<ref name=cling/>

Elsewhere [[Independent (politician)|independent]] Tom Leadley gained a seat from Labour in [[Morley, West Yorkshire|Morley]] North, while the Greens held their seat in [[Wortley, West Yorkshire|Wortley]].<ref name=cling/> [[Voter turnout|Turnout]] in the election dropped to just over 30%<ref>{{cite news| url = https://www.bbc.co.uk/leeds/content/articles/2006/04/26/civic_life_local_elections_2006_feature.shtml | title = Local elections 2006 |date= 27 May 2007| access-date = 6 August 2018|work= [[BBC News Online]]}}</ref> with some wards seeing under 20% voting, while the lowest turnout came in [[Headingley]] at only 15%.<ref name=turned>{{cite news| title = Turned out bad again... |newspaper= [[Yorkshire Evening Post]] | date = 3 May 2003 |issn=0963-2255}}</ref>

Following the election the deputy leader of the council, [[Keith Wakefield]], became leader unopposed after a meeting of the Labour group on the council.<ref name=life>{{cite news| title = Life force |newspaper= [[Yorkshire Evening Post]] | date = 7 May 2003 |issn=0963-2255}}</ref>
