[[File:Wigan80.png|thumb|300px|right|Map of the results of the 1980 Wigan council election.]]

''''All-out' elections to the [[Metropolitan Borough of Wigan|Wigan]] Council''' were held on 1 May 1980, following extensive boundary changes and entirely new wards, yet retaining the number of 24 wards with three seats each for a total of 72 seats. The results were comparable to the [[1973 Wigan Council election|1973 election]] (also an election where all 72 seats were up for vote), with [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] rewarded a crushing majority in seats for approaching 60% of the vote with their main competitors, the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]], falling to under 30%. The [[Liberal Party (UK)|Liberals]] seen their highest representation yet by way of winning all three seats in Langtree.

A former Labour councillor who'd represented the just-abolished ward 19 (encompassing central/north Hindley) since the council's creation fought the [[Hindley, Greater Manchester|Hindley]] ward as an [[Independent Labour]]. Overall turnout fell to the slightly higher than usual number of 36.2% from the general election turnout of 75.7% [[1979 Wigan Council election|last year]], with all wards recording at least one competitor - although that meant in a number of wards Labour were unopposed for one or two of the seats.<ref name="results">{{cite web | url = http://www.wigan.gov.uk/NR/rdonlyres/3692F771-E969-41EE-B068-1F9F20DFE0B7/0/WiganResults1973to2007.pdf | title = Wigan Council results from 1973 to 2008 | publisher = wigan.gov.uk | access-date = 2012-01-30 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20111120022015/http://www.wigan.gov.uk/NR/rdonlyres/3692F771-E969-41EE-B068-1F9F20DFE0B7/0/WiganResults1973to2007.pdf | archive-date = 2011-11-20 | url-status = dead }}</ref>
