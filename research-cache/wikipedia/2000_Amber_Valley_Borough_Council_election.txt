[[File:Amber Valley UK local election 2000 map.svg|thumb|300px|right|Map of the results of the 2000 Amber Valley council election. [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] in blue, [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] in red and [[Independent (politician)|Independent]] in grey.]]
The '''2000 Amber Valley Borough Council election''' took place on 4 May 2000 to elect members of [[Amber Valley]] [[Borough status in the United Kingdom|Borough]] Council in [[Derbyshire]], England. The whole council was up for election with boundary changes since the [[1999 Amber Valley Council election|last election]] in 1999 increasing the number of seats by 2.<ref name="gain">{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/hi/english/static/uk_politics/vote2000/locals/2.stm|title=Amber Valley|publisher=BBC News|access-date=16 May 2011}}</ref> The [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] gained control of the council from the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]].<ref name="council">{{cite news|title=Local council results|date=6 May 2000|work=Financial Times|page=6}}</ref>

==Election result==
Overall [[Voter turnout|turnout]] in the election was 31.6%,<ref name="results">{{cite web|url=http://www.ambervalley.gov.uk/locdem/elections/may2000/results1.asp|title=Election - May 2000|publisher=Ambernet|access-date=16 May 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20020531064959/http://www.ambervalley.gov.uk/locdem/elections/may2000/results1.asp|archive-date=2002-05-31 <!-- 064959 --> }}</ref> with a trial in 2 [[Wards of the United Kingdom|wards]] seeing a 115% increase in [[postal voting]] after anyone was allowed to apply.<ref name="station">{{cite news|title=Making it easy: Experiments to improve the turnout in local elections have had an impact, although some voters still want to use a polling station|last=Wainwright|first=Martin|date=15 May 2000|work=The Guardian|page=17}}</ref>
