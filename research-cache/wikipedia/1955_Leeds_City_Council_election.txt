The '''1955 [[County Borough of Leeds|Leeds]] municipal elections''' were held on Thursday 12 May 1955,<ref name="EJ">{{cite news |title="Little Election" Raises Hopes of Conservatives |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=gyBlAAAAIBAJ&sjid=E4gNAAAAIBAJ&dq=elections%20britain&pg=2570%2C1969170 |newspaper=[[Edmonton Journal]] |date=13 May 1955 |access-date=2 September 2012}}</ref> with one third of the council up for election, as well as a vacancy in Wellington.<ref name="results">{{cite news |title=Municipal results: Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=13 May 1955 }}</ref>

With the third consecutive election producing swings to them - this time a 2.1% swing<ref name="sharpe">{{cite book |first=L.J. |last=Sharpe  |year=1967 |title=Voting in cities: the 1964 borough elections}}</ref> - the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] managed to top the poll and make gains from [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] for the first time since the [[1951 Leeds City Council election|1951 election]], echoing their national result.<ref name="EJ" /> This, however, did not stop Labour winning a comfortable majority of the seats contested. The three gains were in [[Beeston, Leeds|Beeston]], Westfield and [[Wortley, Leeds|Wortley]] - wards where Labour had gained from them following 1951.<ref name="results" /> Party totals remained unchanged with the new division of aldermen effectively wiping out those three gains, as Labour were allotted - at Conservative expense - three more aldermen. Turnout rose marginally to a low figure of 39.4%.<ref name="sharpe" />
