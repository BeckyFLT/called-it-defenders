The '''2022 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election''' took place on 5 May 2022. One third of councillors — 21 out of 63 — were elected. The election took place alongside the [[2022 South Yorkshire mayoral election]] and [[2022 United Kingdom local elections|other local elections across the United Kingdom]].

In the [[2021 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election|previous council election in 2021]], [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] maintained its longstanding control of the council, holding 49 seats after the election. The [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] formed the main opposition with seven seats, with the Conservatives on three, independents and the [[Democrats and Veterans|Democrats and Veterans Party]] having two each and a single Barnsley Independent Group councillor.

== Background ==
[[File:Barnsley UK local election 2018 map.svg|thumb|Result of the council election when these seats were last contested in 2018]]
[[File:Barnsley UK local election 2021 map.svg|thumb|Result of the most recent council election in 2021]]
The [[Local Government Act 1972]] created a two-tier system of metropolitan counties and districts covering [[Greater Manchester]], [[Merseyside]], [[South Yorkshire]], [[Tyne and Wear]], the [[West Midlands (county)|West Midlands]], and [[West Yorkshire]] starting in 1974. Barnsley was a district of the South Yorkshire metropolitan county.<ref name=guide>{{cite book |title=Local Government in England and Wales: A Guide to the New System |year=1974 |publisher=[[HMSO]] |location=London |isbn=0-11-750847-0 |page=7 }}</ref> The [[Local Government Act 1985]] abolished the metropolitan counties, with metropolitan districts taking on most of their powers as [[metropolitan borough]]s. The [[South Yorkshire Combined Authority]] was established in 2014 as the Sheffield City Region Combined Authority, which began electing the [[mayor of South Yorkshire]] in 2018.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Sheffield devolution deal|url=https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sheffield-devolution-deal|access-date=2022-01-22|website=GOV.UK|language=en}}</ref>

Since its formation, Barnsley has continuously been under [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] control. Councillors have predominantly been elected from the Labour Party, various independents, the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] and the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]]. The council elected a large number of Barnsley Independent Group councillors in the [[2006 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2006 election]], whose numbers have been falling since. Councillors who will be seeking re-election will have been most recently elected in the [[2018 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2018 election]], in order to complete a four-year term. In that election, 19 Labour councillors were election alongside one Conservative councillor and one Liberal Democrat councillor. In the [[2021 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election|most recent election in 2021]], seventeen Labour councillors were elected with 40.4% of the vote across the borough, with three Liberal Democrats on 11.8% of the vote across the borough and one Conservative with 25.1% of the vote across the borough. Labour held the Rockingham ward after a coin toss broke a tie between the Labour candidate and the Conservative candidate.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Brooke|first=Sam|date=2021-05-07|title=Local elections Barnsley results with tied seat won by coin toss|url=https://www.examinerlive.co.uk/news/local-news/live-barnsley-local-elections-results-20541997|access-date=2022-01-22|website=YorkshireLive|language=en}}</ref> After that election, the council had 49 Labour councillors, seven Liberal Democrats, three Conservatives, two independents, two [[Democrats and Veterans]] and one Barnsley Independent Group councillor.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Prest|first=Victoria|date=2021-05-06|title=Barnsley council election results 2021: Find out who won|url=https://www.examinerlive.co.uk/news/local-news/barnsley-council-election-results-2021-20541556|access-date=2022-01-22|website=YorkshireLive|language=en}}</ref> The councillor Trevor Smith joined the Labour Party after his previous Democrats and Veterans Party was dissolved.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Councillor faces calls to stand down|url=https://www.barnsleychronicle.com/article/19810/councillor-faces-calls-to-stand-down|access-date=2022-01-22|website=Barnsley Chronicle|language=en}}</ref> 

The positions up for election in 2022 were last [[2018 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election|elected in 2018]], when Labour won 19 seats and the Conservatives and Liberal Democrats won one each.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Barnsley local election results 2018 |url=https://www.barnsley.gov.uk/news/barnsley-local-election-results-2018/ |access-date=2022-03-28 |website=www.barnsley.gov.uk}}</ref>

== Electoral process ==

The council elects its councillors in thirds, with a third being up for election every year for three years, with no election in the fourth year.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.gov.uk/guidance/local-government-structure-and-elections#electoral-cycles|title=Local government structure and elections|work=GOV.UK|access-date=2018-04-27|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/694598/Election_Timetable_in_England.pdf|title=Election Timetable in England}}</ref> The election will take place by [[first-past-the-post voting]], with wards generally being represented by three councillors, with one elected in each election year to serve a four-year term.

All [[Elections in the United Kingdom#Registration procedure|registered electors]] ([[British citizen|British]], [[Irish citizen|Irish]], [[Commonwealth citizen|Commonwealth]] and [[European Union]] citizens) living in Barnsley aged 18 or over will be entitled to vote in the election. People who live at two addresses in different councils, such as university students with different term-time and holiday addresses, are entitled to be registered for and vote in elections in both local authorities. Voting in-person at polling stations will take place from 07:00 to 22:00 on election day, and voters will be able to apply for postal votes or proxy votes in advance of the election.
