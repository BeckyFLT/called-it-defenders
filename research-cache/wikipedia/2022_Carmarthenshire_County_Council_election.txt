}}The '''2022 Carmarthenshire County Council election''' took place on Thursday 5 May 2022<ref>{{Cite web|title=Local Government and Elections (Wales) Act 2021|url=https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/home/council-democracy/councillors-ams-and-mps/how-to-become-a-councillor/#.YerBTHrP2Uk|url-status=live|website=legislation.gov.uk|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210422223337/https://www.carmarthenshire.gov.wales/home/council-democracy/councillors-ams-and-mps/how-to-become-a-councillor/ |archive-date=2021-04-22 }}</ref> to elect 75 members to [[Carmarthenshire County Council|Carmarthenshire Council]]. On the same day, elections were held to the other 21 local authorities and to community councils in Wales as part of the [[2022 Welsh local elections]].

Plaid Cymru won a majority of seats for the first time, whilst losing their leader, Emlyn Dole. <ref>{{Cite news|title=Leader of Carmarthenshire Council Emlyn Dole loses his seat|language=en-GB|work=Wales Online|url=https://www.walesonline.co.uk/news/wales-news/leader-carmarthenshire-council-plaid-emlyn-23887830|access-date=2022-05-24}}</ref> Welsh Labour regained some ground in Carmarthenshire by winning seats in the Amman Valley and Llanelli constituency from Plaid Cymru. New Independent lost one seat overall and their leader, Jeff Edmunds and the Conservatives' only councillor, Shahana Najmi, also lost her seat. 

Despite winning a majority of seats, Plaid Cymru formed a coalition with eight independent councillors.

== Background ==
Council elections in Wales were originally scheduled for May 2021, but were delayed to avoid a conflict with the [[2021 Senedd election]]. The frequency of the elections was also increased from 4 years to five years to avoid future clashes, meaning (after 2022) the next council election is expected in 2027.

The number of councillors were increased by 1 after the [[Local Democracy and Boundary Commission for Wales]] recommended the change in a report in August 2019.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Carmarthenshire Final Recommendations {{!}} LDBC|url=https://ldbc.gov.wales/reviews/08-19/carmarthenshire-final-recommendations|access-date=2022-01-21|website=ldbc.gov.wales|date=21 August 2019 }}</ref> Although there was an increase from 74 to 75 seats in the council, the number of wards were decreased to 51 from 58.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|title=Carmarthenshire to have one more councillor following electoral boundaries review|url=https://www.southwalesguardian.co.uk/news/19431715.carmarthenshire-one-councillor-following-electoral-boundaries-review/|access-date=2022-01-21|website=South Wales Guardian|date=10 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref>

The Plaid Cymru group in conjunction with Independent group had been in control of the council since May 2015.<ref>{{Cite news|date=2015-05-13|title=New coalition takes over Carmarthenshire council|language=en-GB|work=BBC News|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-wales-south-west-wales-32728342|access-date=2022-01-21}}</ref> Labour claimed to be confident of success, though had suffered a number of resignations from the party since the last election. Many former Labour members were standing in this election as New Independents.<ref>{{Cite news|title=Carmarthenshire Labour confident of taking council administration with aid of Independents|url=https://walesnewsonline.com/carmarthenshire-labour-confident-of-taking-council-administration-with-aid-of-independents/|date=4 May 2022|access-date=2022-07-22|work=Wales News Online|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220722105012/https://walesnewsonline.com/carmarthenshire-labour-confident-of-taking-council-administration-with-aid-of-independents/|archive-date=22 July 2022|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Boundary changes ==
In July 2021 Welsh Government accepted a number of ward change proposals by the [[Local Democracy and Boundary Commission for Wales]], with slight modifications on Welsh language names for some wards. These are to take effect from the 2022 council election.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Written Statement: Local Authority Electoral Boundary Review – Update (8 July 2021)|url=https://gov.wales/written-statement-local-authority-electoral-boundary-review-update-1|access-date=2022-01-21|website=GOV.WALES|date=8 July 2021 |language=en}}</ref>  The changes gave a better parity of representation. Thirty-four wards remained unchanged.<ref name=":0" /> Of the other wards:<ref>{{Cite web|date=July 2019|title=Review of the Electoral Arrangements of the County of Carmarthenshire|url=https://ldbc.gov.wales/sites/ldbc/files/review/Carmarthenshire%20Final%20Report_e_reduced_4.pdf|url-status=live|website=Local Democracy and Boundary Commission for Wales|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220121173750/https://ldbc.gov.wales/sites/ldbc/files/review/Carmarthenshire%20Final%20Report_e_reduced_4.pdf |archive-date=2022-01-21 }}</ref>

* [[Carmarthen Town North]] ward merges with [[Carmarthen Town South]] ward and is renamed "Carmarthen Town North and South", with total decrease in councillors from 4 to 3.
* [[Ammanford]] ward merges with Pontamman ward with a 2 councillor seat, and renamed "Ammanford".
* Tycroes community of Llanedi Ward merges with Llanedi community of Hendy Ward with 1 councillor, and renamed "Tycroes".
* [[Hendy]] community of Hendy ward becomes a ward of its self with 1 councillor.
* Bynea ward would have an increase from 1 to 2 councillors.
* [[Dafen, Carmarthenshire|Dafen]] ward merges with [[Felinfoel]] ward with a 2 councillor seat, and renamed "Dafen and Felinfoel".
* [[Bigyn (electoral ward)|Bigyn]] ward would have an increase from 2 to 3 councillors.
* Tyisha ward merges with a part of Elli Ward (known as 'Lakefield' – Polling district TYI-B) with 2 councillors, and named "Tyisha".
* Elli ward loses area 'Lakefield' (Polling district TYI-B) and remains with 1 councillor.
* [[Kidwelly]] ward merges with [[St Ishmaels|St. Ishmael]] town of [[St Ishmaels|St. Ishmael]] ward with a 2 councillor seat, and renamed "Kidwelly and St. Ishmael".
* [[Llangyndeyrn]] ward merges with [[Llandyfaelog]] community of [[St Ishmaels|St. Ishmael]] ward with a 2 councillor seat, and renamed "Llangyndeyrn".
* [[Llansteffan]] ward merges with [[St Clears|St. Clears]] ward with a 2 councillor seat, and renamed "St. Clears with Llansteffan".
* [[Trelech]] ward merges with [[Newchurch and Merthyr]] community of [[Cynwyl Elfed]] ward, remains with a 1 councillor seat, and named "Trelech".
* [[Cynwyl Elfed]] ward loses [[Newchurch and Merthyr]] community, remains with a 1 councillor seat, and named "Cynwyl Elfed".
* [[Cenarth]] ward merges with [[Llangeler]] ward with a 2 councillor seat, and renamed "Cenarth and Llangeler".
* [[Cilycwm (electoral ward)|Cilycwm]] ward merges with [[Cynwyl Gaeo]] and [[Llanycrwys]] communities of [[Cynwyl Gaeo (electoral ward)|Cynwyl Gaeo ward]], with 1 councillor seat, and named "Cilycwm".
* [[Manordeilo and Salem]] ward merges with [[Llansawel]] community of [[Cynwyl Gaeo (electoral ward)|Cynwyl Gaeo ward]], with 1 councillor seat, and named "Manordeilo and Salem".
