The '''1946 [[County Borough of Leeds|Leeds]] municipal elections''' were held on Saturday 2 November 1946,<ref name="NYT">{{cite news |title=Laborites Lead in British Election|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1946/11/02/archives/laborites-in-lead-in-british-election.html |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=2 November 1946 |accessdate=16 October 2012}}</ref> with one third of the council and vacancies in [[Burmantofts]] and [[Farnley, Leeds|Farnley]] & [[Wortley, Leeds|Wortley]] to be elected. A handful of wards - [[Armley]] & Wortley, Burmantofts, [[Holbeck]] North, [[Hunslet]] Carr & [[Middleton, Leeds|Middleton]] and [[Osmondthorpe]] - went uncontested.<ref name="results">{{cite news |title=Municipal results: Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=3 November 1946}}</ref>

Rebounding from the heavy defeat [[1945 Leeds City Council election|the year before]], the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] managed a 13% swing<ref name="sharpe">{{cite book |first=L.J. |last=Sharpe  |year=1967 |title=Voting in cities: the 1964 borough elections}}</ref> to win the popular vote - although that feat went poorly rewarded, as [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] won a comfortable majority of the seats contested and made a net gain to add to their national victory.<ref name="NYT" /><ref name="results" /> Elsewhere the [[Liberal Party (UK)|Liberal]] vote was less than half of that the previous year, and the [[Communist Party of Great Britain|Communists]] contested this year with three candidates. Turnout seen a slight drop from the prior election to 41.9%.<ref name="sharpe"/>
