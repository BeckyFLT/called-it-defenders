[[File:Leeds UK local election 1992 map.png|right|thumb|500px|Map of the results for the 1992 Leeds council election.]]
The '''1992 [[Leeds City Council]] elections''' were held on Thursday, 7 May 1992, with one third of the council's seats up for election.

Held one month after the [[1992 United Kingdom general election|1992 general election]] where [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] won a surprise fourth term, the Conservatives in Leeds shared in that success, surpassing [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour's]] vote for the first time in a decade, and winning their greatest share since [[1978 Leeds City Council election|1978]]. Despite these feats, their vote remained constant with recent elections - in stark contrast to the opposition parties, whose votes had seen dramatic falls, with the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrat]] vote their lowest since 1978, and Labour's their lowest recorded since the council's creation in [[1973 Leeds City Council election|1973]].

The minor parties also saw disappointment, with the [[Liberal Party (UK, 1989)|Liberal]] vote more than halved the [[1991 Leeds City Council election|previous year]] and comfortably their worst, whilst the [[Green Party of England and Wales|Greens]] - in spite of a record number of candidates, up considerably from the year before - seen a decrease in their vote. All of which resulted in a record low in turnout, suggesting the Tory revival was driven more by their national result hurting opposition moral than any resurgence in their support.

The Conservatives were able to win their greatest number of wards since [[1983 Leeds City Council election|1983]], gaining a seat each from Labour and the Lib Dems in their respective marginals of [[Morley, West Yorkshire|Morley North]] and [[Horsforth (ward)|Horsforth]]. The Tory gain in Horsforth, as well as Labour's rise in [[Armley (ward)|Armley]], left the Lib Dem victories confined to just two wards for the first time since [[1979 Leeds City Council election|1979]] ([[Burmantofts]] and [[Moortown (ward)|Moortown]]), and their councillor total the lowest since [[1980 Leeds City Council election|1980]]. The sole [[Independent (politician)|Independent]] standing, Peter Kersting, won his fourth term in Pudsey South with ease (with the first being won as Conservative).<ref name="results">{{cite news |title=District results: Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=8 May 1992}}</ref>
