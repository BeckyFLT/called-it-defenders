[[File:Wigan95.png|thumb|500px|right|Map of the results of the 2000 Wigan council election.]]
'''Elections to [[Metropolitan Borough of Wigan|Wigan]] Borough Council''' were held on 4 May 2000. One-third of the council was up for election, as well as an extra vacancy in Norley - both of which were uncontested. Since the election, there had been a [[1999 Wigan Council election#By-elections between 1999 and 2000|by-election]] in which the [[Liberal Democrat (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] gained the seat being fought in Hindsford from [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]].

The [[1999 Wigan Council election|previous year]] had seen a peak in candidates contesting, but with the absence of the [[Green Party of England and Wales|Greens]], who had fielded a full slate last time around, candidates returned to a normal level. Some of those who'd stood for the Greens decided to run on an independent list, covering a quarter of the wards. There were a further handful of [[Independent (politician)|Independent]] candidates elsewhere, including the return of John Vickers in Hindley Green after an eight-year absence, and a second attempt of Jack Sumner in Leigh East, to produce the highest number of Independents contesting since [[1973 Wigan Council election|1973]]. The [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] fielded their highest amount, at 21, since [[1982 Wigan Council election|1982]], and the Lib Dems - whilst still much reduced from their [[SDP-Liberal Alliance|Alliance]] years - produced a stronger showing than recent years of ten candidates. Labour, as usual, contested every seat.

Turnout followed recent trends, at a slightly improved 19.5%, although the three wards that trailed [[postal voting]] seen marked increases.<ref>{{cite web| url = http://www.wiganmbc.gov.uk/pub/cexec/elections/postrep.htm | title = Local Elections 2000 - Report on Postal Ballot Pilot Scheme | publisher = wiganmbc.gov.uk |access-date = 2012-06-29 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20000925073806/http://www.wiganmbc.gov.uk/pub/cexec/elections/postrep.htm | archive-date=2000-09-25}}</ref> Similarities ended there, as the Conservatives seen a dramatic recovery in their fortunes, with their voter share rising to the highest since [[1980 Wigan Council election|1980]], and their vote near double recent years figures. Labour, in turn, suffered double-digit swings against them in most wards, with a sharp fall in their vote share to pre-peak levels and their lowest vote figure since the council's inception, narrowly surpassing the previous figure set in [[1975 Wigan Council election|1975]]. The Lib Dems seen another year of modest improvement in their vote, and the two returning Independents, as well as a third in [[Hindley, Greater Manchester|Hindley]] garnered large swings towards them.

In all, there were two gains on the night, with Labour gaining in Beech Hill to return it to full Labour representation after a decade of Lib Dem inroads, and a loss to the Conservatives who won back representation on the council by way of their first win in [[Orrell, Greater Manchester|Orrell]] since 1982. The Lib Dems narrowly held onto their newly won seat in Hindsford, with a 56-vote majority.<ref>{{cite news| url = http://news.bbc.co.uk/hi/english/static/uk_politics/vote2000/locals/143.stm | title = Wigan | access-date = 2009-12-17 | work = [[BBC News Online]]}}</ref><ref name="results">{{cite web | url = http://www.wigan.gov.uk/NR/rdonlyres/3692F771-E969-41EE-B068-1F9F20DFE0B7/0/WiganResults1973to2007.pdf | title = Wigan Council results from 1973 to 2008 | publisher = wigan.gov.uk | access-date = 2012-01-30 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20111120022015/http://www.wigan.gov.uk/NR/rdonlyres/3692F771-E969-41EE-B068-1F9F20DFE0B7/0/WiganResults1973to2007.pdf | archive-date = 20 November 2011 | url-status = dead}}</ref>
