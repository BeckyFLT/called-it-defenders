The '''2026 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election''' was held on Thursday 7 May 2026 to elect a third of [[Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council]]. It was held [[2026 United Kingdom local elections|alongside council elections across England]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.birminghammail.co.uk/black-country/leaders-look-ahead-first-council-32457732|title=Labour and Conservative leaders set out their big Dudley Council election issues|first=Martyn|last=Smith|date=September 13, 2025|website=Birmingham Live}}</ref>

Prior to the election, the council was under [[no overall control]]. The [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] were the largest party and ran the council as a minority administration.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Smith |first1=Martyn |title=Council remains Conservative-led after deal struck |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6pypj4gy7ro |access-date=30 August 2026 |work=BBC News |date=15 May 2024}}</ref> At this election, the council remained under no overall control. The Conservatives lost seats, but remained the largest party. [[Reform UK]] won 22 of the 25 seats being contested at this election, and overtook [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] to become the second largest party on the council.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Reid |first1=Nick |title=Reform UK make huge gains to become second biggest party on Dudley Council in Local Elections 2026 |url=https://www.itv.com/news/central/2026-05-07/live-updates-as-votes-are-counted-in-dudley-for-local-elections-2026 |access-date=30 August 2026 |work=ITV News |date=8 May 2026}}</ref> The Conservative minority administration continued after the election. The incumbent Conservative leader of the council, Patrick Harley, was reappointed to the role at the subsequent annual council meeting on 28 May 2026, defeating an attempt by Reform to remove him from the post.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Andrews |first1=Mark |title=New cabinet announced as Tories retain leadership of Dudley Council, despite gains by Reform |url=https://www.expressandstar.com/news/dudley/new-cabinet-announced-as-tories-retain-leadership-of-dudley-council-despite-gains-by-reform-8647922 |access-date=30 August 2026 |work=Express and Star |date=29 May 2026}}</ref>

== Background ==

=== History ===
The [[Local Government Act 1972]] created a two-tier system of metropolitan counties and districts covering [[Greater Manchester]], [[Merseyside]], [[South Yorkshire]], [[Tyne and Wear]], the [[West Midlands (county)|West Midlands]], and [[West Yorkshire]] starting in 1974. Dudley was a district of the West Midlands metropolitan county.<ref name=guide>{{cite book |title=Local Government in England and Wales: A Guide to the New System |year=1974 |publisher=[[HMSO]] |location=London |isbn=0-11-750847-0 |page=7 }}</ref> The [[Local Government Act 1985]] abolished the metropolitan county councils, with metropolitan districts taking on most of their powers as [[metropolitan borough]]s. The [[West Midlands Combined Authority]] was created in 2016 and began electing the [[mayor of the West Midlands]] from 2017, which was given strategic powers covering a region coterminous with the West Midlands metropolitan county.<ref>{{cite web|title=CONSTITUTION OF THE WEST MIDLANDS COMBINED AUTHORITY|url=https://www.wmca.org.uk/media/1576/constitution-master-module-2642017.pdf|access-date=4 December 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220531185344/https://www.wmca.org.uk/media/1576/constitution-master-module-2642017.pdf|archive-date=31 May 2022|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Dudley Council has variously been under Labour control, Conservative control and [[no overall control]] since it was established. The Conservatives controlled the council from the [[2004 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2004 election]] until Labour gained control in the [[2012 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2012 election]]. Labour lost overall control in the [[2016 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2016 election]] but continued to lead the council until 2017, when the Conservatives led the council, still without a majority. In the [[2021 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2021 elections]], the Conservatives gained a majority on the council, which they kept in the [[2023 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|2023 election]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Farrington |first=Dayna |title=Full election results from Dudley Council as Conservatives gain power |url=https://www.expressandstar.com/news/politics/elections-west-midlands-mayor-pcc-council-2021/2021/05/07/dudley-councils-election-results--in-full/ |access-date=2022-03-26 |website=www.expressandstar.com |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Dudley result - Local Elections 2023 |language=en-GB |work=BBC News |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/election/2023/england/councils/E08000027 |access-date=2024-01-02}}</ref>

The Conservatives lost control of the council in the [[2024 Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council election|most recent election in 2024]], with no party having an overall majority and Labour and the Conservatives ending up with the same number of seats.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2024-05-03 |title="Dudley Council election results in full: Tories lose control as they end up level with Labour" |url=https://www.expressandstar.com/news/local-hubs/dudley/2024/05/03/dudley-council-election-results-as-first-wards-are-announced/ |url-status= |work=Express and Star}}</ref> After a week of negotiations between the Conservative and Labour groups, it was announced that the existing Conservative leader, Patrick Harley, would remain leader of the council (and therefore appoint the council's cabinet) while Labour would be in charge of the majority of the council's scrutiny committee chairmanships and the mayoralty.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6pypj4gy7ro|title=Council remains Conservative-led after deal struck|date=15 May 2024|access-date=24 May 2024|first1=Martyn|publisher=[[BBC News]]|last1=Smith}}</ref>

In 2025, six Labour councillors including former Labour group leader Pete Lowe left the party to form the [[Black Country Party]].<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |date=2025-05-30 |title=Dudley 'Diggers' form new political party to 'be a voice for the alternative' |url=https://www.dudleynews.co.uk/news/25203846.dudley-diggers-councillors-set-new-black-country-party/ |access-date=2025-06-01 |website=Dudley News |language=en}}</ref>

=== Electoral process ===
The council elects members in thirds every year except the 4th in a four-year cycle.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=Composition of Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council |url=http://opencouncildata.co.uk/council.php?c=270&y=0 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240102150918/http://opencouncildata.co.uk/council.php?c=270&y=0 |archive-date=2 Jan 2024 |access-date=2 Jan 2024 |website=Open Councill Data UK}}</ref> Due to a boundary review of the wards by the [[Local Government Boundary Commission for England]], all 72 seats to [[Dudley Metropolitan Borough Council]] were up in the previous election in 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Local Government Boundary Review |url=https://www.dudley.gov.uk/council-community/elections/local-government-boundary-review/ |access-date=2024-01-02 |website=www.dudley.gov.uk |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=LGBCE |title=Dudley {{!}} LGBCE |url=https://www.lgbce.org.uk/all-reviews/dudley |access-date=2024-01-02 |website=www.lgbce.org.uk |language=en}}</ref>
