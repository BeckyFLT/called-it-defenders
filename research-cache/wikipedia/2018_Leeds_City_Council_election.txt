The '''2018 Leeds City Council election''' took place on Thursday 3 May 2018 to elect members of [[Leeds City Council]] in England.<ref name="Results_040518">{{cite web | title = Election results | url = https://www.leeds.gov.uk/your-council/elections/election-results | website = leeds.gov.uk | publisher = [[Leeds City Council]] | date = 4 May 2018 | access-date = 7 May 2018 | archive-date = 9 November 2020 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20201109010020/https://www.leeds.gov.uk/your-council/elections/election-results | url-status = live }}</ref> It was held on the same day as other [[2018 United Kingdom local elections|UK local elections across England]].

Following a full [[boundary review]] of [[Leeds|Leeds']] 33 [[electoral wards]] by the [[Local Government Boundary Commission for England|Local Government Boundary Commission]], the all-out election saw all of the council's 99 available council seats contested based on the new ward boundaries. Three of the previous wards were abolished and replaced ([[Leeds city centre|City]] & [[Hunslet]], [[Headingley]], and [[Hyde Park, Leeds|Hyde Park]] & [[Woodhouse, Leeds|Woodhouse]] for [[Headingley and Hyde Park (ward)|Headingley & Hyde Park]], [[Hunslet and Riverside (ward)|Hunslet & Riverside]] and [[Little London and Woodhouse (ward)|Little London & Woodhouse]]). The [[2004 Leeds City Council election|last all-out election in Leeds was in 2004]] after the previous full ward boundary review in 2003.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.lgbce.org.uk/all-reviews/yorkshire-and-the-humber/west-yorkshire/leeds|title=LGBCE &#124; Leeds &#124; LGBCE Site|access-date=2 June 2018|archive-date=25 May 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180525232556/http://www.lgbce.org.uk/all-reviews/yorkshire-and-the-humber/west-yorkshire/leeds|url-status=live}}</ref>

With three seats available for each ward, electors were able to cast up to three votes for three different candidates. The first three candidates [[First past the post|past the post]] in each ward won a council seat.<ref>Jeremy Morton, '[https://www.southleedslife.com/use-your-three-votes-in-leeds-city-council-elections/ Use your three votes in Leeds City Council elections] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200911033227/https://southleedslife.com/use-your-three-votes-in-leeds-city-council-elections/ |date=11 September 2020 }}', ''South Leeds Life'' (2 May 2018).</ref>

The [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]] won the election with 61 of the 99 council seats.

Incumbent [[Morley Borough Independent]] councillor, Robert Finnigan, did not stand in the ward he represented, [[Morley North (ward)|Morley North]]. Instead, he stood in the neighbouring ward of [[Morley South (ward)|Morley South Ward]]. However, Finnigan was subsequently not elected at the election for the ward.

==Ward results==
Three councillors were elected for each of the wards.

An asterisk (*) denotes an incumbent councillor who stood again at the election, having 21 sitting councillors not stood again for their seats.

The '''percentage vote share (%)''' is calculated by counting only the highest-scoring candidate for each party and individual independent candidates. For example, the total number of votes cast by electors in the [[Adel and Wharfedale (ward)|Adel & Wharfedale ward]] for Barry Anderson (the highest-scoring Conservative candidate), Nigel Gill (Labour), Peter Jackson (Liberal Democrat) and Liddy Swales (sole Green candidate) was 7,980. As Anderson gained 4,856 votes, he took 61.5% of the 7,980 total possible ballots cast, whilst Gill gained 1,556 votes and 19.7% of the total votes cast.

The '''percentage change (±)''' is the proportion by which the individual party and/or candidate's vote share increased or decreased from the [[2016 Leeds City Council election|previous council election in 2016]].

The '''turnout''' is the amount of registered electors who voted in the ward at the time of the election. The '''turnout percentage (%)''' is the proportion of registered electors in the ward who voted on the day of the election.
