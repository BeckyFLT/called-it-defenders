The '''2026 Lambeth London Borough Council election''' took place on 7 May 2026, as part of the [[2026 United Kingdom local elections]]. All 63 members of [[Lambeth London Borough Council]] were elected. The election took place alongside [[2026 London local elections|local elections in the other London boroughs]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Coughlan |first=Joe |date=2025-04-30 |title=London local elections, why they aren't in 2025 and when the next ones will be |url=https://www.mylondon.news/news/uk-world-news/london-local-elections-arent-any-31429137 |access-date=5 September 2025 |work=My London }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Local authority, combined authority, and county combined authority election cycles in England |url=https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/election-timetable-in-england/election-timetable-in-england|date=22 July 2025|publisher=Department for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities |access-date=5 September 2025 |via=GOV.UK}}</ref> 

Prior to the election, the council was under [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] majority control. At the election, the council went under [[no overall control]]. The [[Green Party of England and Wales|Green Party]] overtook Labour to become the largest party, but fell three seats short of a majority. They chose Martin Abrams to be the new leader of the Green group on 13 May.<ref name="Abrams"/> He was subsequently formally appointed as the new leader of the council on 1 June 2026.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2026/06/breaking-news-extraordinary-meeting-of-the-council-underway/|date=1 June 2026|work=Brixton Buzz|title=Abrams voted new leader of Lambeth Council at Extraordinary Meeting|last=Ross|first=Phil|accessdate=2 June 2026}}</ref>

==Background==
{{See also|Lambeth London Borough Council elections}}
=== History ===
[[File:Lambeth London UK local election 2022 map.svg|thumb|Result of the 2022 election]]
The thirty-two [[London boroughs]] were established in 1965 by the [[London Government Act 1963]]. They are the principal authorities in Greater London and have responsibilities including education, housing, planning, highways, social services, libraries, recreation, waste, environmental health and revenue collection. Some of the powers are shared with the [[Greater London Authority]], which also manages passenger transport, police and fire.<ref>{{cite web|title=The essential guide to London local government|publisher=London Councils|url=https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/who-runs-london/essential-guide-london-local-government|access-date=10 October 2021}}{{deadlink|date=May 2026}}</ref>

Since its formation, [[Lambeth London Borough Council|Lambeth]] has generally been under [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] control apart from one period from 1968 to 1971 of [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] control and several periods of [[no overall control]]. The council was controlled by a [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrat]]-[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] [[Coalition government|coalition]] from 2002 to 2006, and from 2006 until this election continuously had a [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] majority.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://southwarknews.co.uk/area/south-london/election-results-lambeth-council-falls-under-no-clear-control/|title=Election results: Lambeth council falls under no clear control|date=11 May 2026|first=Frankie|last=Hills|work=Southwark News|accessdate=21 May 2026}}</ref>

==Changes since 2022==
===By-elections===
Following the 2022 elections, the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]] secured a majority on Lambeth Council with 58 seats. The [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] formed the official opposition with three seats, while the [[Green Party of England and Wales|Green Party]] won two.

A by-election in the Vauxhall ward was held in October 2023, following the death of Labour councillor Liam Jarnecki. Labour retained the seat, with Tom Swaine-Jameson.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.mylondon.news/news/south-london-news/labour-wins-south-london-council-27855271|title=Labour wins South London council by-election after previous councillor died of cancer|date=6 October 2023|first=Robert|last=Firth|work=MyLondon|accessdate=21 May 2026|publisher=Reach}}</ref>

Two by-elections took place in May 2024. In Knight’s Hill, Labour councillor Sonia Winifred resigned after having the [[party whip]] suspended for defying it in a Gaza ceasefire vote.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/labour-party-lambeth-council-whip-gaza-ceasefire-vote-b1141958.html|title=Anger as councillors have Labour whip suspended after voting for Gaza ceasefire|first=Lydia|last=Chantler-Hicks|date=28 February 2024|work=The Standard|accessdate=9 May 2026}}</ref> Labour held the seat, with Emma Nye elected. On the same day, a by-election was held in Streatham Common and Vale following the resignation of [[Tom Rutland]], who stepped down to focus on contesting [[East Worthing and Shoreham]]. Labour retained the seat, electing Sarah Cole.

A further by-election in Streatham Common and Vale took place in July 2024 after the resignation of Hena Shah. The seat was again held by Labour, with Dominic Armstrong elected.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.mylondon.news/news/south-london-news/labour-wins-streatham-common-vale-29478956|title=Labour wins Streatham Common and Vale council by-election|date=5 July 2024|work=MyLondon|first=Robert|last=Firth|publisher=Reach|accessdate=21 May 2026}}</ref>

In May 2025, a by-election was held in Herne Hill and Loughborough Junction following the election of [[Jim Dickson (politician)|Jim Dickson]] as MP for [[Dartford (UK Parliament constituency)|Dartford]]. The seat was gained by the Green Party, with Paul Valentine elected, making the Greens the joint opposition party on the council.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2025/05/greens-edge-out-labour-in-herne-hill-and-loughborough-junction-upset-as-valentine-claims-council-seat/|title=Greens edge out Labour in Herne Hill and Loughborough Junction upset as Valentine claims Council seat|first=Jason|last=Cobb|date=2 May 2025|work=Brixton Buzz|accessdate=9 May 2026}}</ref>

===Affiliation changes===
Cllr Martin Abrams resigned from the Labour Party in August 2025, highlighting a culture of alleged bullying and factionalism. He was suspended by Lambeth Labour in early 2024 after voting for a ceasefire motion in Gaza.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://insidecroydon.com/2025/08/29/jewish-councillor-quits-labour-over-bullying-and-anti-semitism/|title=Jewish councillor quits Labour claiming he has been bullied|date=29 August 2025|work=Inside Croydon|accessdate=9 May 2026}}</ref> He joined the Greens in September 2025, making them the official opposition group on Lambeth Council.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2025/09/ex-labour-councillor-martin-abrams-defects-to-lambeth-greens-party-now-becomes-official-opposition-at-town-hall/|title=Ex-Labour councillor Martin Abrams defects to Lambeth Greens – party now becomes official opposition at Town Hall|first=Jason|last=Cobb|date=11 September 2025|work=Brixton Buzz|accessdate=9 May 2026}}</ref>

In July 2025, Cllr Irfan Mohammed was suspended from Labour after being charged with a criminal offences relating to exposure, controlling behaviour and sexual assault.<ref>{{cite web | title=UPDATE: Full details emerge after Lambeth Labour councillor Irfan Mohammed charged with controlling behaviour, sexual assault and exposure | url=https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2025/07/update-full-details-emerge-after-lambeth-labour-councillor-irfan-mohammed-charged-with-controlling-behaviour-sexual-assault-and-exposure/|work=Brixton Buzz|date=17 July 2025|first=Jason|last=Cobb|accessdate=21 May 2026 }}</ref> He sat as an Independent.<ref>{{cite web | title=Lambeth Labour councillor charged and suspended as party moves to limit fallout | url=https://www.brixtonbuzz.com/2025/07/lambeth-labour-councillor-charged-and-suspended-as-party-moves-to-limit-fallout/|work=Brixton Buzz|first=Jason|last=Cobb|accessdate=21 May 2026 }}</ref>

In November 2025, Cllr Christiana (Tina) Valcarcel, defected to the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]], making them the joint opposition party on the council.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Burford |first=Rachael |date=12 November 2025 |title=London Labour sees more defections as support for Keir Starmer’s party plunges |url=https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/london-labour-defections-councillors-keir-starmer-lambeth-b1257406.html |access-date=20 November 2025|work=The Standard}}</ref>

==Electoral process==
Lambeth, as is the case all other London borough councils, elects all of its councillors at once every four years, with the previous election having taken place in 2022. The election takes place by multi-member [[first-past-the-post voting]], with each ward being represented by two or three councillors. Electors will have as many votes as there are councillors to be elected in their ward, with the top two or three being elected.

All [[Elections in the United Kingdom#Registration procedure|registered electors]] ([[British citizen|British]], [[Irish citizen|Irish]], [[Commonwealth citizen|Commonwealth]] and [[European Union]] citizens) living in London aged 18 or over are entitled to vote in the election. People who live at two addresses in different councils, such as university students with different term-time and holiday addresses, are entitled to be registered for and vote in elections in both local authorities.<ref name=":1">{{cite web|title=How the elections work|publisher=London Councils|url=https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/who-runs-london/london-elections/how-elections-work|access-date=14 September 2021}}{{deadlink|date=May 2026}}</ref> Voting in-person at polling stations takes place from 7:00 to 22:00 on election day, and voters are able to apply for postal votes or proxy votes in advance of the election.<ref name=":1" />

=== Greens ===
During the campaign, the [[Green Party of England and Wales|Green Party]] faced controversy over its candidate in [[Streatham St Leonard's]] ward on [[Lambeth London Borough Council|Lambeth Council]] — the party's strongest ward in the borough, where all three sitting councillors are Green. Saiqa Ali came under scrutiny after ''[[The Spectator]]'' reported on social media posts attributed to her Instagram account. The posts included an image of a masked, armed fighter described as wearing a Hamas headband, captioned "Long live the Resistance"; a depiction of the Earth being crushed by a serpent bearing the Star of David; and a repost of a mural widely condemned as antisemitic, which had previously drawn criticism when Jeremy Corbyn was found to have engaged with it in 2018. Following contact from ''The Spectator'', Ali's Instagram account was set to private. Questions were also raised about whether Ali met the residency or workplace requirements to stand in Lambeth, as her nomination papers listed a home address in the neighbouring borough of Croydon.<ref>{{cite web |last=Gilligan |first=Andrew |date=15 April 2026|title=The hateful posts of yet another Green party candidate |url=https://spectator.com/article/the-hateful-posts-of-yet-another-green-party-candidate/ |access-date=22 April 2026|work=The Spectator}}</ref>

Separately, Sabine Mairey, the party's candidate in [[Clapham Town|Clapham Town ward]], was reported to have shared a Facebook post describing an attack on a synagogue as "not antisemitism" but "revenge," referencing a vehicle-ramming incident at a synagogue in Michigan earlier in the year.<ref>{{cite news |last=Walker |first=Peter|date=3 May 2026 |title=Green candidate seen canvassing in London after arrest over alleged antisemitism |url=https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/may/03/green-candidate-sabine-mairey-social-media-posts-canvassing-london |access-date=11 May 2026|work=The Guardian |issn=0261-3077}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Green candidate campaigns in London after being arrested for alleged anti-Semitism |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/politics/2026/05/03/green-candidate-campaigns-london-alleged-anti-semitism/ |access-date=11 May 2026|work=The Telegraph|first=Emily|last=Smith|date=3 May 2026}}{{subscription}}</ref> [[Mark Bittlestone]], a comedian and history teacher also standing in [[Clapham Town]], was found by the ''[[Daily Mail]]'' to have reposted material suggesting the [[October 7 attacks]] were a covert "false flag," and to have shared posts from other users describing Israel as a "colony of inbreds, rapists and thieves" and claiming it had no right to exist. [[Mark Bittlestone|Bittlestone]] deleted all his social media profiles following the exposé.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Bennett |first=Steve |title=Mark Bittlestone fails in his council bid|date=9 May 2026|work=Chortle : The UK Comedy Guide |url=https://www.chortle.co.uk/news/2026/05/09/60563/mark_bittlestone_fails_in_his_council_bid |access-date=11 May 2026 }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |date=5 May 2026 |title=UK Labour Party releases video ft. antisemitic Green Party candidates|url=https://www.jpost.com/diaspora/antisemitism/article-895219 |access-date=11 May 2026 |work=Jerusalem Post|first=Mathilda|last=Heller}}</ref> Both Ali and Mairey were subsequently arrested by the [[Metropolitan Police]] on suspicion of stirring up racial hatred online, an offence under section 19 of the [[Public Order Act 1986]]. Neither candidate appeared on the Lambeth Green Party's website thereafter, and the party said the posts did not reflect its views.{{Citation needed|date=May 2026}}
