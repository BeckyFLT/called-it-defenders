The '''1995 [[Leeds City Council]] elections''' were held on Thursday, 4 May 1995, with one third of the council up for election, alongside a vacancy in [[Roundhay]].

[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] won another victory over the opposition parties, winning a record number of wards as the Labour gains extended further into [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] heartland. A disastrous result for the Tories saw them fall even further from the record lows they set [[1994 Leeds City Council election|the year before]], losing [[Cookridge]], [[Alwoodley (ward)#Councillors since 1980|North]] and [[Roundhay]] for the first time - with [[Wetherby]] their sole defence. Labour gained eight in total, securing second councillors in the previously reliable Conservative wards of [[Aireborough]], [[Temple Newsam (ward)#Councillors since 1973|Halton]], [[Pudsey|Pudsey North]] and [[Weetwood]]. As a result, Labour represented over three-quarters of the council with a formidable majority of 51.

Having overtaken the Conservative vote the previous year, the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Lib Dems]] fell behind again, but unlike the Conservatives were able to defend their three seats. As such, the holding up of the Labour's vote from strong figure achieved the year before was enough to win them their greatest share, amidst a poor turnout of 32.5%.<ref name=crest>{{Cite web|url=http://www.crest.ox.ac.uk/election/econ99tab.htm |title=Local Election Results, 1999 |access-date=2010-02-08 |publisher=Centre for Research into Elections and Social Trends |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110724053301/http://www.crest.ox.ac.uk/election/econ99tab.htm |archive-date=2011-07-24 }}</ref>

Elsewhere, the [[Green Party of England and Wales|Greens]] firmed up their second place in [[Wortley, Leeds|Wortley]] and increased their slate to cover half of the wards, whilst the [[Liberal Party (UK, 1989)|Liberals]] fielded one fewer to match the number of [[Independents (politician)|Independents]] - all three of which making little impact. The leftist splinters from Labour, [[Socialist Party (England and Wales)|Militant Labour]], and the now-defunct [[CPGB]], [[Communist Party of Britain]] also fielded a first candidate each.<ref name="results">{{cite news |title=District results: Leeds |work=[[The Yorkshire Post]] |date=5 May 1995}}</ref>
