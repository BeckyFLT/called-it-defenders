The '''2011 Barnsley Metropolitan Borough Council election''' took place on 5 May 2011 to elect members of [[Metropolitan Borough of Barnsley|Barnsley]] [[Metropolitan Borough]] Council in [[South Yorkshire]], England. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]] stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name="hold">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/special/election2011/council/html/cc.stm|title=England council elections|publisher=BBC News|access-date=21 June 2011| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110719151726/http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/special/election2011/council/html/cc.stm| archive-date= 19 July 2011 | url-status= live}}</ref>

After the election, the composition of the council was:
*[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] 43
*Barnsley Independent Group 13
*[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] 6
*[[Independent (politician)|Independent]] 1<ref name="full">{{cite news|url=http://www.barnsley-chronicle.co.uk/news/article/3969|title=Labour's majority up to 23 – full election results here|date=6 May 2011|work=Barnsley Chronicle|access-date=21 June 2011}}</ref>

==Election result==
The results saw Labour make 5 gains from the Barnsley Independent Group to have 43 [[councillor]]s and a 23-vote majority.<ref name=full/> The Barnsley Independent Group dropped to 13 seats,<ref name=full/> while the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] stayed on 6 seats<ref name="doncaster">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-south-yorkshire-13304378|title=Labour holds Doncaster, Rotherham and Barnsley councils|date=6 May 2011|publisher=BBC News|access-date=22 June 2011| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110519210730/http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-south-yorkshire-13304378| archive-date= 19 May 2011 | url-status= live}}</ref> and there remained 1 other [[Independent (politician)|independent]].<ref name=full/> Labour defeated sitting councillors for the Barnsley Independent Group in [[Hoyland]] Milton, Kingstone, Old Town and Rockingham [[Wards of the United Kingdom|wards]].<ref name=full/> They also gained [[Darfield, South Yorkshire|Darfield]] where the sitting councillor had stood down at the election, with the successful Labour candidate being a former [[Member of the European Parliament]] [[Brian Key]].<ref name=full/> The Labour leader of the council, Steve Houghton, called on his party not to be complacent after their gains, while the leader of the Barnsley Independent Group called the results "a bitter blow".<ref name="reflect">{{cite news|url=http://www.barnsley-chronicle.co.uk/news/article/3977|title=Leaders reflect on election result|last=Bradford|first=Lynsey|date=6 May 2011|work=Barnsley Chronicle|access-date=22 June 2011}}</ref>

Among other results, Jane Collins for the [[United Kingdom Independence Party]], following her second place in the [[2011 Barnsley Central by-election]], came third in [[Monk Bretton]] ward.<ref name="disappointment">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-13525938|title=Yorkshire's smaller parties look ahead following election disappointment|last=Tringle|first=Len|date=27 May 2011|publisher=BBC News|access-date=22 June 2011| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110618130405/http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-13525938| archive-date= 18 June 2011 | url-status= live}}</ref> Meanwhile, the [[British National Party]], after putting up 19 candidates, failed to come close to taking any seats.<ref name=disappointment/> Overall [[Voter turnout|turnout]] in the election was 36.3%.<ref name="elections">{{cite web|url=http://www.wearebarnsley.com/2011/05/05/elections-result-and-opinion/ |title=Elections – Result and Opinion |date=5 May 2011 |publisher=wearebarnsley.com |access-date=21 June 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120325234001/http://www.wearebarnsley.com/2011/05/05/elections-result-and-opinion/ |archive-date=25 March 2012 }}</ref>

Councillors elected in 2007 ended their term in 2011. The change in vote shares in compared to this corresponding election.
