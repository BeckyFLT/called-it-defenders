The '''1998 Tunbridge Wells Borough Council election''' took place on 7 May 1998 to elect members of [[Tunbridge Wells (borough)|Tunbridge Wells]] [[Borough status in the United Kingdom|Borough]] Council in [[Kent]], [[England]]. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative party]] gained overall control of the council from [[no overall control]].<ref name=policy>{{cite news | title = Policy and politics: Local Elections: Analysis: Council poll results | publisher = [[The Guardian]] | page = 16 | date = 1998-05-09}}</ref>

After the election, the composition of the council was
*[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] 27
*[[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrat]] 12
*[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] 7
*[[Independent (politician)|Independent]] 2<ref name=policy/>

==Campaign==
Before the election the council was run by the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] after depriving the Conservatives of a majority in 1995 and winning majority control in 1996.<ref name=disgusted>{{cite news | first = Stuart | last = Miller | title = 'Disgusted' fights a losing battle in old stronghold Tunbridge Wells embodied Tory values until 1995. But the party's popularity is so low it is unlikely to recapture its old bastion | publisher = [[The Guardian]] | page = 12 | date = 1998-04-24}}</ref> However the Conservatives were optimistic of making gains as they were no longer in government nationally and after some Liberal Democrat [[councillor]]s had defected.<ref name=disgusted/> The Liberal Democrats however were confident of continuing to run the council, with the national party leader [[Paddy Ashdown]] visiting the area to campaign for the party.<ref name=disgusted/> Meanwhile, [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] were not seen as being important in the election, with the party not contesting all of the [[Wards of the United Kingdom|wards]].<ref name=disgusted/>

Issues in the election included plans for [[traffic calming]] and to widen the [[A21 road (England)|A21]].<ref name=disgusted/> The Liberal Democrat council's plans to create cycle and [[bus lane]]s by using part of the [[Common land|common]] were attacked by the Conservatives.<ref name=heartland>{{cite news | first = Helen | last = Carter | title = Policy and politics: Local Elections: Tonic for the Tories as heartland spa town returns to the fold Tunbridge Wells | publisher = [[The Guardian]] | page = 15 | date = 1998-05-09}}</ref>

==Results==
The results saw the Conservatives win control of the council after winning all 12 seats that the Liberal Democrats had been defending.<ref name=heartland/> The victory was seen as being symbolic for the Conservatives in the [[1998 United Kingdom local elections|1998 local elections]] as an area which they had lost at the height of unpopularity in the mid-1990s.<ref name=hague>{{cite news | first = Frank | last = Millar | title = Hague hails Tory comeback in polls | publisher = [[The Irish Times]] | page = 14 | date = 1998-05-09}}</ref>
