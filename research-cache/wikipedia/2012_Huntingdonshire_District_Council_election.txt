The '''2012 Huntingdonshire District Council election''' took place on 3 May 2012 to elect members of [[Huntingdonshire]] [[Non-metropolitan district|District]] Council in [[Cambridgeshire]], England. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name="vote2012">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/special/vote2012/council/E07000011.stm|title=Huntingdonshire|date=4 May 2012|publisher=BBC News|access-date=12 July 2014}}</ref>

After the election, the composition of the council was:
*[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] 39
*[[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] 7
*[[UK Independence Party]] 3
*[[Independent (politician)|Independent]] 2
*[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] 1<ref name="cambridgeshire">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-cambridgeshire-17921739|title=Local election gains for Labour in Cambridgeshire|date=4 May 2012|publisher=BBC News|access-date=12 July 2014}}</ref>

==Background==
Before the election the Conservatives controlled the council with 41 seats, compared to 8 for the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]], 2 [[UK Independence Party]] and 1 [[Independent (politician)|independent]].<ref name="clock">{{cite news|url=http://www.cambridge-news.co.uk/Huntingdon-St-Ives-St-Neots/Clock-ticks-down-to-districts-elections-12042012.htm|title=Clock ticks down to district's elections|last=Makey|first=Julian|date=12 April 2012|work=News & Crier|access-date=12 July 2014}}</ref> 3 [[councillor]]s stood down at the election, Conservatives Philip Swales of Ramsey [[Wards and electoral divisions of the United Kingdom|ward]] and John Watt in [[Yaxley, Cambridgeshire|Yaxley]] and [[Farcet]], as well as Liberal Democrat Peter Ward in [[Warboys]] and [[Bury, Cambridgeshire|Bury]].<ref name="door">{{cite news|title=Door is open for new faces|last=Raville|first=Matthew|date=2 May 2012|work=[[Peterborough Evening Telegraph]]|via=NewsBank}}</ref>

15 seats were contested with the Conservatives guaranteed to keep a majority on the council whatever happened.<ref name=clock/> The Conservatives, [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]], Liberal Democrats and UK Independence Party contesting every seat except for in [[Sawtry]] where there was no Conservative candidate and [[Ramsey, Cambridgeshire|Ramsey]] where the Liberal Democrats did not stand.<ref name="thursday">{{cite news|url=http://www.huntspost.co.uk/news/latest-news/hunts_goes_to_the_polls_thursday_1_1365895|title=Hunts goes to the polls Thursday|date=2 May 2012|work=Hunts Post|access-date=12 July 2014}}</ref> There was one candidate from the [[Official Monster Raving Loony Party]] and a number of independents standing, including Dick Tuplin defending his seat in Sawtry, former Liberal Democrat Derek Giles in [[St Neots]] [[Eaton Socon]] and 2 candidates opposed to the westward expansion of [[St Ives, Cambridgeshire|St Ives]].<ref name=thursday/>

==Election result==
The Conservatives lost 3 seats and were run close in [[Godmanchester]] and The Hemingfords as well,<ref name="hdc">{{cite news|url=http://www.huntspost.co.uk/news/latest-news/hdc_full_poll_results_1_1368852|title=HDC – full poll results|date=4 May 2012|work=Hunts Post|access-date=12 July 2014}}</ref> but they kept a strong majority on the council.<ref name="dominate">{{cite news|url=http://www.cambridge-news.co.uk/News/Labour-celebrates-Huntingdonshire-milestone-but-Tories-still-dominate-04052012.htm|title=Slideshow: Labour celebrates Huntingdonshire milestone – but Tories still dominate|date=4 May 2012|work=Cambridge News|access-date=12 July 2014}}</ref> Labour, the UK Independence Party and an independent each took a seat from the Conservatives, but the Conservatives did gain a seat in Warboys and Bury from the Liberal Democrats.<ref name=dominate/>

The Labour gain came in [[Huntingdon]] North, where Patrick Kadewere defeated the chairman of the council Jeff Dutton.<ref name="third">{{cite news|title=UKIP take third ward seat in Ramsey|date=4 May 2012|work=[[Peterborough Evening Telegraph]]|via=NewsBank}}</ref> This gave Labour their first seat on the council since the [[1999 Huntingdonshire District Council election|1999 election]].<ref name=dominate/> Meanwhile, the UK Independence Party took a seat in Ramsey, giving the party all 3 councillors for the ward,<ref name=third/> with the party also getting an increased share of the vote across the council.<ref name=hdc/>

Independents polled strongly with former Liberal Democrat councillor Derek Giles getting back on the council as an independent after taking St Neots Eaton Socon from the Conservatives.<ref name=hdc/> Independent Dick Tuplin also held his seat in Sawtry 
with a majority of 818 votes,<ref name=third/> while other independents came close to taking seats.<ref name=hdc/><ref name=dominate/>

The Liberal Democrats lost one of the three seats they had been defending and came last in many of the wards they had been contesting.<ref name=hdc/> However party leader Peter Downes held his seat in [[Brampton, Cambridgeshire|Brampton]] comfortably and the party held Huntingdon East from the Conservatives after a recount.<ref name=hdc/>
