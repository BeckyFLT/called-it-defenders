The '''2011 North Somerset Council election''' took place on 5 May 2011 to elect members of [[North Somerset]] [[Unitary authority|Unitary]] Council in [[Somerset]], [[England]]. The whole council was up for election and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] stayed in overall control of the council.<ref name="hold">{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/special/election2011/council/html/hc.stm|title=England council elections|publisher=[[BBC News]]|access-date=20 August 2011}}</ref>

==Background==
At the [[2007 North Somerset Council election|last election in 2007]] the Conservatives gained control of the council with 46 seats.<ref name="gain">{{cite news|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/shared/vote2007/councils/html/hc.stm|title=North Somerset|publisher=[[BBC News]]|access-date=20 August 2011}}</ref> This was compared to 6 [[Independent (politician)|independents]], 5 [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]], 3 [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] and 1 [[Green Party of England and Wales|Green Party]] [[councillor]]s.<ref name=gain/>

In February 2011 Liberal Democrat councillor for [[Nailsea]] East Andy Cole said he would be standing in the election as an independent.<ref name="turns">{{cite news|url=http://www.thewestonmercury.co.uk/news/liberal_democrat_councillor_turns_independent_1_802378|title=Liberal Democrat councillor turns independent|last=Angear|first=Vicky|date=15 February 2011|work=Weston, Worle & Somerset Mercury|access-date=20 August 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110217035109/http://www.thewestonmercury.co.uk/news/liberal_democrat_councillor_turns_independent_1_802378|archive-date=17 February 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref> Cole was one of a total of 182 candidates who stood in the election for the 61 seats on the council.<ref name="unveiled">{{cite news|url=http://www.thewestonmercury.co.uk/news/election_north_somerset_council_candidates_unveiled_1_854989|title=Election: North Somerset Council candidates unveiled|date=5 April 2011|work=Weston, Worle & Somerset Mercury|access-date=20 August 2011}}</ref> These were 58 Conservatives, 53 Labour, 42 Liberal Democrats, 20 independents, 5 Green Party, 3 [[United Kingdom Independence Party]] and 1 from the All The South Party.<ref name=unveiled/> The most candidates were in [[Weston-super-Mare]] West [[Wards of the United Kingdom|ward]], where 12 people contested the seats.<ref name=unveiled/>

The Conservative administration of the previous 4 years was attacked over the cost moving the council offices to Castlewood in [[Clevedon]] and over the refurbishment of the [[town hall]] in Weston-super-Mare.<ref name="fortress">{{cite news|url=http://www.thisisbristol.co.uk/Tory-fortress-threatened-broad-coalition-opponents/story-11305113-detail/story.html|title=Tory fortress would only be threatened by broad coalition of opponents|date=7 April 2011|publisher=this is Bristol|access-date=20 August 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120924143329/http://www.thisisbristol.co.uk/Tory-fortress-threatened-broad-coalition-opponents/story-11305113-detail/story.html|archive-date=24 September 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> However the Conservatives said they had been able to make savings and therefore not had to make as big cuts as other councils.<ref name="grip">{{cite news|url=http://www.thisisbristol.co.uk/Tories-grip-power/story-11282980-detail/story.html|title=Conservatives keep grip on power on North Somerset Council|date=7 May 2011|publisher=This is Bristol|access-date=20 August 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110817192810/http://www.thisisbristol.co.uk/Tories-grip-power/story-11282980-detail/story.html|archive-date=17 August 2011|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Election result==
The results saw the Conservatives remain in control of the council after winning 42 of the 61 seats. Independents won 7 seats, the Liberal Democrats 6, Labour 5 and the Green Party 1 seat.<ref name=grip/>

The Liberal Democrats took 2 seats from the Conservatives in Weston-super-Mare Central<ref name=grip/> and 3 seats from them in Weston-super-Mare West,<ref name="clevedon">{{cite news|url=http://www.clevedonpeople.co.uk/groups/north-somerset-council/Clevedon-votes-stick-Tories-District-Councillors/story-11089835-detail/story.html|title=Clevedon votes to stick with the Tories: District Councillors election results|date=6 May 2011|publisher=Clevedon People|access-date=20 August 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120330235710/http://www.clevedonpeople.co.uk/groups/north-somerset-council/Clevedon-votes-stick-Tories-District-Councillors/story-11089835-detail/story.html|archive-date=30 March 2012|url-status=usurped}}</ref> but lost 3 seats back to the Conservatives in Weston-super-Mare South [[Worle]].<ref name=grip/> Meanwhile, Labour also gained 2 seats from the Conservatives in Weston-super-Mare East.<ref name=clevedon/> Long time councillor and independent Nan Kirsen lost her seat in [[Pill, Somerset|Pill]] to another independent Don Davies, standing for the Sustainable Pill and District Party, by 17 votes, while Andy Cole won his seat in Nailsea East as an independent after leaving the Liberal Democrats.<ref name=grip/>
