'''Elections to [[Lambeth London Borough Council]]''' were held on 2 May 2002. The whole council was up for election with boundary changes reducing the number of councillors by one since the last election in [[1998 Lambeth Council election|1998]].<ref name=lose>{{cite news| url = https://news.bbc.co.uk/hi/english/static/vote2002/local_elections/82.stm | title = Lambeth | access-date = 2009-08-15 | work = [[BBC News Online]]}}</ref> [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] despite having the largest number of votes with 36.6% of the vote, it still lost 13 seats, while the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] and the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative Party]] gained seats, resulting in [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] losing control of the Council and no party having a majority.<ref name=lose/>

Following the election, the Liberal Democrats and Conservatives formed a coalition to run the council with Cllr Peter Truesdale, Liberal Democrat, as Leader and Cllr John Whelan, Conservative, as Deputy Leader.
