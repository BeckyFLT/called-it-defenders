The '''2000 Oldham Council election''' took place on 4 May 2000 to elect members of [[Metropolitan Borough of Oldham|Oldham]] [[Metropolitan Borough]] Council in [[Greater Manchester]], England. One third of the council was up for election and the [[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrats]] gained overall control of the council from the [[Labour Party (UK)|Labour Party]].<ref name=gain>{{cite news| url = https://news.bbc.co.uk/hi/english/static/uk_politics/vote2000/locals/81.stm | title = Oldham | access-date = 10 April 2010 | work = [[BBC News Online]]}}</ref>

After the election, the composition of the council was:
*[[Liberal Democrats (UK)|Liberal Democrat]] 31
*[[Labour Party (UK)|Labour]] 25
*[[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservative]] 2
*[[Green Party of England and Wales|Green]] 2<ref name=councils>{{cite news| title = Election results: local councils | newspaper = [[The Times]] | page = 4 | date = 5 May 2000 }}</ref>

==Campaign==
Before the election Labour ran the council with 32 seats compared to 25 for the Liberal Democrats who were the main opposition party on the council.<ref name=heartland>{{cite news| first = Alan | last = Pike | title = Poll fears linger, despite Blair's pat on the back: Doubts over its heartland vote mean that Labour faces real concerns in next week's local elections. | newspaper = [[Financial Times]] | page = 6 | date = 27 April 2000 }}</ref> However the [[1999 Oldham Council election|1999 election]] had seen the Liberal Democrats win more votes than Labour, 42.5% compared to 38%, and they were confident of at least depriving Labour of a majority on the council in the election.<ref name=heartland/>

Issues in the election included crime, education, employment, health and litter.<ref name=defection>{{cite news| title = Battlground Oldham: Disillusionment and defection may be deciders | newspaper = [[Financial Times]] | page = 6 | date = 5 May 2000 }}</ref> [[Unemployment]] was down at around 5%, but still above the national average and was much higher in some of the inner city areas.<ref name=defection/>

In the week before the election Labour suffered a blow when a [[councillor]], Margaret Kelly, defected to the [[Green Party of England and Wales|Green Party]].<ref name=defection/> She had been a Labour member for 30 years, but said that the national Labour government was not what she had fought for in opposition.<ref name=defection/>

==Election result==
The results saw the Liberal Democrats gain control of the council from the Labour party.<ref name=jubilant>{{cite news| first = Russell | last = Jenkins | title = Lib Dems jubilant at taking Oldham | newspaper = [[The Times]] | page = 4 | date = 5 May 2000 }}</ref> Labour, who had run the council for the previous 20 years, suffered a number of losses including the council leader John Battye in [[Failsworth]] East [[Wards of the United Kingdom|ward]].<ref name=jubilant/> Battye, who had been leader of the council for the previous 15 years, was defeated by 1,605 votes by a 23-year-old Liberal Democrat candidate Charles Glover in the most high-profile contest in the election.<ref name=change>{{cite news| first = Caroline | last = Cheetham | title = Council stunned as leader Battye is defeated: Time for a change after 20-year rule | newspaper = [[Manchester Evening News]] | page = 29 | date = 5 May 2000 }}</ref>

The swing to the Liberal Democrats was over 8% since 1996,<ref name=defection/> enabling them to gain control of the council for the first time.<ref name=change/> They now had an overall majority of 2,<ref name=morale>{{cite news| title = Conservative revival beats expectations: Local elections results deliver 'substantial blow to Labour morale' | newspaper = [[Financial Times]] | page = 6 | date = 5 May 2000 }}</ref> after they gained seats in [[Coldhurst]], [[Hollinwood, Greater Manchester|Hollinwood]], [[Royton]] North and Royton South in addition to Failsworth East.<ref name=change/> The Liberal Democrats also regained a seat in Crompton, where the independent Liberal Democrat councillor, Michael Hambley, stood down at the election.<ref name=change/> Meanwhile, the [[Conservative Party (UK)|Conservatives]] doubled the number of seats they held on the council to 2, after gaining a seat in [[Chadderton]] North from Labour.<ref name=change/>

The national Liberal Democrat leader, [[Charles Kennedy]] described the results in Oldham as a "fantastic result, showing the inroads that Liberal Democrats were making into Labour's heartlands".<ref name=kennedy>{{cite news| title = Kennedy leadership boosted by strong results; Lib Dems: | newspaper = [[Financial Times]] | page = 6 | date = 5 May 2000 }}</ref> However the local Labour [[Member of Parliament]] [[Phil Woolas]] said that the election was down to local issues and had "nothing to do with the national political situation".<ref name=kennedy/>

Following the election there were allegations of [[Electoral fraud|vote rigging]] in the election.<ref name=rigging>{{cite news| first = John | last = Innes | title = Eleven guilty of rigging vote in Oldham election | newspaper = [[The Scotsman]] | page = 4 | date = 31 July 2001 }}</ref> After a police investigation, 11 people were convicted of election fraud in July 2001.<ref name=rigging/> They were convicted after police found evidence of voters using dead people's names to vote and impersonating other voters.<ref name=rigging/> The offences had taken place in the wards of the Coldhurst, St Mary's and [[Werneth, Greater Manchester|Werneth]] and those convicted were both Labour and Liberal Democrat supporters.<ref name=rigging/> Another [[defendant]], Liberal Democrat councillor Mohib Uddin, who had been elected in Coldhurst, was [[Acquittal|acquitted]].<ref name=rigging/>
