The '''2019 [[Redcar and Cleveland]] [[Borough]] Council [[election]]''' took place on 2 May 2019 to elect members of [[Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council]] in [[England]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Election results |url=https://www.redcar-cleveland.gov.uk/Democracy/previous-election-results/2019%20Local%20Elections/Local%20Election%20Results.pdf |website=Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council |access-date=5 August 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190521224118/https://www.redcar-cleveland.gov.uk/Democracy/previous-election-results/2019%20Local%20Elections/Local%20Election%20Results.pdf |archive-date=21 May 2019}}</ref> This was on the same day as other [[2019 United Kingdom local elections|local elections]].

The council was under [[no overall control]] prior to the election, being run by a Labour minority administration. Following the election the council remained under no overall control, but a coalition of the Liberal Democrats and independent councillors formed to take control, with independent councillor [[Mary Lanigan]] being appointed the new leader of the council.<ref>{{cite news |title=Redcar and Cleveland Council to be run by Lib Dems and independents |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-tees-48264477 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=BBC News |date=14 May 2019}}</ref>

==Changes 2019–2023==
Local party the Teesville Independents was de-registered with the [[Electoral Commission (United Kingdom)|Electoral Commission]] in June 2019; its councillors thereafter sat as independents.<ref>{{cite web |title=Eston Independent Party |url=https://search.electoralcommission.org.uk/English/Registrations/PP901 |website=The Electoral Commission |access-date=22 February 2024}}</ref>

The two UKIP councillors, Mike Lockwood and Christopher Gallacher, both left the party in October 2020 to sit as independents.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Boothroyd |first1=David |title=Poor leadership in Conwy |url=https://www.localcouncils.co.uk/2020/10/poor-leadership-in-conwy/ |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Local Councils |publisher=Thorncliffe |date=23 October 2020}}</ref>

Six Conservatives left the party in early 2021 to sit as independents, forming the "Cleveland Independent Group". They were Cliff Foggo, Julie Craig, Graham Jeffrey, Vera Rider, Philip Thompson, and Malcolm Griffiths (the latter had been suspended from the party since November 2019).<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title=Blow for Tories as councillors resign to sit as independents 'with integrity and not with duplicity' |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/blow-tories-councillors-resign-sit-20252998?ref=BNTMedia&utm_medium=facebook |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=25 March 2021}}</ref> In December 2021 Graham Jeffrey left the Cleveland Independent Group to join the Liberal Democrats, and Cliff Foggo, Julie Craig, and Vera Rider left the group to join the Independent Group, leaving just Philip Thompson and Malcolm Griffiths in the Cleveland Independent Group.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Boothroyd |first1=David |title=...and a happy New Forest |url=https://www.localcouncils.co.uk/2021/12/and-a-happy-new-forest/ |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Local Councils |publisher=Thorncliffe |date=24 December 2021}}</ref> Julie Craig subsequently left the Independent Group in March 2022 and served the remainder of her term as an ungrouped independent.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title='Hokey cokey' claim as Redcar and Cleveland councillor is ousted from cabinet |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/hokey-cokey-claim-redcar-cleveland-23549627 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=30 March 2022}}</ref>

Craig Hannaway, elected for Labour, left the party in March 2021 to sit as an independent.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title=Labour councillor overlooked for Hartlepool by-election 'thrown out of party' after critical tweets |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/labour-councillor-overlooked-hartlepool-election-20247353 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=24 March 2021}}</ref>

Longbeck by-election 6 May 2021 won by Conservative candidate Andrea Turner, following death of Conservative councillor Norah Cooney.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Teale |first1=Andrew |title=Longbeck Ward - Redcar and Cleveland |url=https://www.andrewteale.me.uk/leap/ward/20674/#2021-05-06 |website=Local Elections Archive Project |access-date=22 February 2024}}</ref>

Guisborough by-election 6 May 2021 won by Conservative candidate Andrew Hixon, following death of Conservative councillor Dennis Teasdale.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Teale |first1=Andrew |title=Guisborough Ward - Redcar and Cleveland |url=https://www.andrewteale.me.uk/leap/ward/20669/#2021-05-06 |website=Local Elections Archive Project |access-date=22 February 2024}}</ref>

Hutton by-election 6 May 2021 won by Conservative candidate Stephen Waterfield, following resignation of Conservative councillor Caroline Jackson.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Teale |first1=Andrew |title=Hutton Ward - Redcar and Cleveland |url=https://www.andrewteale.me.uk/leap/ward/20670/#2021-05-06 |website=Local Elections Archive Project |access-date=22 February 2024}}</ref>

Adam Brook, elected as an independent, joined Labour in July 2022.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title=Independent councillor on Redcar and Cleveland Council defects to Labour Party |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/independent-councillor-redcar-cleveland-council-24487943 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=14 July 2022}}</ref>

Normanby by-election 22 December 2022 won by Conservative candidate Paul Salvin, following death of Labour councillor Chris Foley-McCormack.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title=Tories triumph in local by-election held after councillor's death |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/tories-triumph-local-election-held-25825171 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=23 December 2022}}</ref>

Robert Clark, elected as a Teesville Independent, joined Labour in January 2023.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title=Second independent Redcar and Cleveland councillor defects to Labour |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/second-independent-redcar-cleveland-councillor-26018570 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=19 January 2023}}</ref>

==The Independent Group==
The independent councillors who formed part of the council's administration with the Liberal Democrats sat together as "The Independent Group". This was a group for the purposes of the Local Government (Committees and Political Groups) Regulations 1990 (and therefore used to determine entitlement to seats on committees) but was not a formal political party registered with the Electoral Commission.<ref>{{cite legislation UK|type=si|si=The Local Government (Committees and Political Groups) Regulations 1990|year=1990|number=1553|access-date=22 February 2024}}</ref> Some members of the group did register a new political party in December 2021, called "Redcar and Cleveland Independent",<ref>{{cite news |last1=Arnold |first1=Stuart |title=New political association in Redcar and Cleveland aiming to build 'fighting fund' for next elections |url=https://www.gazettelive.co.uk/news/teesside-news/new-political-association-redcar-cleveland-22685062 |access-date=22 February 2024 |work=Teesside Live |date=9 January 2022}}</ref> but the party was de-registered less than a year later without ever having contested an election.<ref>{{cite web |title=Redcar and Cleveland Independent |url=https://search.electoralcommission.org.uk/English/Registrations/PP12970 |website=The Electoral Commission |access-date=22 February 2024}}</ref> Those members of the Independent Group who stood for re-election at the subsequent 2023 election did so as independent candidates.
